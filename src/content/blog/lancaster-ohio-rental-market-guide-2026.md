Here is what the numbers say for investors weighing the Fairfield County seat in 2026.*

TL;DR

The Lancaster Ohio rental market offers lower entry prices than Columbus: listing data from September 2026 puts typical rent around $1,250 to $1,300, and three-bedroom rents mostly fall between $1,560 and $1,780. Home prices run roughly $235,000 to $275,000. Cash flow is realistic, but about 7 in 10 homes were built before 1980, so maintenance capital matters more here than in newer suburbs. Columbus-only rental rules do not apply.

Key Takeaways

- Lancaster’s median gross rent ($1,083) is about 16% below Columbus’s ($1,295), per Census ACS 2020 to 2024 estimates.
- A median-priced Lancaster home rented at $1,700 a month produces an illustrative gross yield of roughly 7.4% to 8.7%, but falls short of the 1% rule.
- Roughly 71% of Lancaster’s housing units were built before 1980, with a median construction year of 1963.
- Columbus’s rental registry, Pay to Stay, source-of-income, and rent-increase notice rules stop at the Columbus city limits and do not reach Lancaster.
- Evictions in Lancaster run through Fairfield County Municipal Court, which lists a $140 filing fee and sets trial about 21 days after filing.

## Why Lancaster Attracts Renters

Lancaster is the county seat and largest city in Fairfield County, with an estimated [41,956 residents as of July 2025](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/lancastercityohio). It has its own hospital, college campus, courthouse, and factories, so many tenants work in town rather than commuting to Columbus.

### Affordability relative to the Columbus core

Census data shows the gap plainly. Lancaster’s median gross rent is [$1,083](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/lancastercityohio) against [$1,295 in Columbus](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/columbuscityohio) (ACS 2020 to 2024), and the median owner-occupied home value is $193,900 versus $252,900. Median household income is lower too: $59,133 in Lancaster compared with $66,082 in Columbus. The poverty rate, though, is lower in Lancaster (12.6% vs. 18.1%).

### Jobs, institutions, and the US-33 corridor

[Fairfield Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Ohio) is the city’s largest employer, followed by Anchor Hocking, Fairfield County government, and Lancaster City Schools. Anchor Hocking’s Pierce Avenue plant is the company’s remaining glass plant after it [closed its Charleroi, Pennsylvania operation in 2024](https://www.glass-international.com/news/us-glassware-plant-set-to-close-with-325-jobs-impacted) and shifted work to Lancaster. [Ohio University Lancaster](https://www.ohio.edu/iea/student-data/enrollment/regionalenrollment) reported a fall 2025 headcount of 2,457.

Newer employers are arriving on the city’s west side. Google is building a data center at [105 Whiley Road, west of US-33](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2023/05/08/google-is-building-two-more-data-centers-in-central-ohio/), and Cirba Solutions announced a [$200 million-plus battery recycling expansion with 150 new jobs](https://www.cirbasolutions.com/cirba-solutions-u-s-department-of-energy-celebrate-expansion-of-lithium-ion-battery-recycling-operations-in-ohio/) in 2023. Treat both as modest demand support. Data centers employ relatively few permanent staff, and 150 jobs will not move citywide rents on their own.

For Columbus commuters, US-33 is the lifeline. The Lancaster bypass opened as a [limited-access freeway in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_33_in_Ohio), and the drive runs through Canal Winchester to I-270. Residents report an average commute of [27.7 minutes](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/lancaster-oh).

### Place and housing character

Downtown centers on Main and Broad Streets inside the [Lancaster Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_Historic_District_(Lancaster,_Ohio)), listed on the National Register in 1983 and home to the Sherman House Museum and the Square 13 blocks. [Rising Park](https://www.fairfieldcountyparks.com/rising_park.html) on East Fair Avenue, with its 250-foot Mount Pleasant sandstone bluff, sits next to the Fairfield County Fairgrounds. Walkable brick streets downtown and postwar neighborhoods farther out draw different tenants, which widens the pool across property types.

## How Long Does It Take to Lease a Lancaster Rental?

No public source publishes a reliable Lancaster-only rental vacancy rate, so use two proxies.

First, residents move less often. In ACS 2020 to 2024 data, [85.3% of Lancaster residents](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/lancastercityohio) lived in the same house a year earlier, compared with [80.3% in Columbus](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/columbuscityohio). Fewer moves means fewer turnovers, but also fewer renters shopping at any given moment. About 41% of Lancaster households rent, well below the roughly 56% renter share in Columbus.

Second, the seasonal curve is visible in the data. Zumper showed Lancaster rents down 3% month over month in September 2026, and Redfin’s median dropped 6.6% over the same stretch. That matches the pattern across Central Ohio: the strongest leasing window runs from late spring through August, and listings that hit the market in November or December tend to sit longer or need a sharper price.

Plan on 4 to 6 weeks to lease once a home is rent-ready, which is the range RL Property Management uses for its own budgeting. ## What Investors Should Know Before Buying

### The cash-flow math (illustrative)

This example uses the sourced medians above. It is illustrative, not a pro forma.

| Line Item | City Median | ZIP 43130 Median |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $235,000 | $274,000 |
| Monthly rent (3BR, mid-range) | $1,700 | $1,700 |
| Annual gross rent | $20,400 | $20,400 |
| Gross yield | 8.7% | 7.4% |
| Monthly rent as % of price (1% rule) | 0.72% | 0.62% |
| Est. property tax (before credits) | ~$3,190/yr | ~$3,720/yr |
| Rent after tax and $137/mo management | ~$15,570 (6.6% of price) | ~$15,040 (5.5% of price) |

That last line excludes insurance, repairs, vacancy, reserves, and financing. The tax estimate applies the tax year 2025 effective residential rate for the Lancaster City/Lancaster City Schools district (38.75 mills in district 53, per the [Fairfield County Auditor’s TY2025 levy report](https://www.co.fairfield.oh.us/auditor/pdf/TY2025-Levies.pdf)) to 35% of market value. Two other Lancaster tax districts carry lower effective rates (about 34.5 to 35.0 mills), so check the parcel’s actual bill.

To hit the 1% rule at $1,700 a month, you’d need a purchase price near $170,000. In Lancaster, that price usually buys an older, smaller house, which leads to the next point.

### Older housing stock needs more capital

According to ACS estimates compiled by [Point2Homes](https://www.point2homes.com/US/Neighborhood/OH/Fairfield-County/Lancaster-Demographics.html), about 24.6% of Lancaster’s housing units were built in 1939 or earlier and roughly 71% before 1980. The median year built is 1963. Less than 1% dates from 2020 or later.

> **Roughly 7 in 10 Lancaster homes were built before 1980. Budget for the house you’re buying, not the rent you’re hoping for.**

Expect galvanized or cast-iron plumbing, older electrical panels, stone or block foundations, and lead-based paint in many pre-1978 homes. A 1920s foursquare near downtown can rent well, but it will ask for more at each turn than a 1990s ranch. RLPM’s standard guidance of a 6-month rent reserve (about $10,200 on a $1,700 house) is a floor here, not a ceiling. RLPM does not handle renovation projects, so buyers of a house that needs a major rehab should line up a contractor first; Lancaster requires most contractors to register with the city under [Codified Ordinances Chapter 1341](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/lancaster/latest/lancaster_oh/0-0-0-72765). More on sizing reserves: [how much to keep in a rental property reserve fund](/blog/property-reserve-fund-rental/).

### The tenant pool is different

A 3x income screen is a useful lens. Lancaster’s $59,133 median household income supports rent of about $1,640 a month at 3x. Three-bedroom houses at $1,700 to $1,800 sit above what the median household qualifies for, so the applicant pool for top-end houses is narrower than in Gahanna or Powell. Pricing near the middle of the range brings more qualified applicants.

> **Lancaster’s median household income supports about $1,640 in rent under a 3x screen. Price above that and the applicant pool thins.**

School district is part of the pitch for families. Lancaster City Schools serves most of the city, but some addresses in ZIP 43130 fall into surrounding districts such as Berne Union, so confirm the district by parcel on the [Fairfield County Auditor’s site](https://auditorrealestate.fairfieldcountyohio.gov/) before you list.

For an out-of-state owner, Lancaster’s 33-mile distance from Columbus is a real factor. A self-managing owner in Dublin or Westerville faces an hour-plus round trip per showing or repair. ## Rental Compliance Considerations in Lancaster

As of October 2026, Lancaster rentals follow Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law and the city has no rental registry on the books. This section is informational, not legal advice; consult an Ohio attorney on specific situations.

### Columbus rules that do not apply

Several rules Columbus owners deal with stop at the Columbus city limits:

- The [Columbus rental registry](/blog/columbus-rental-property-registry/) (CCC Chapter 4515, registration opening October 1, 2026)
- Columbus Pay to Stay (CCC 4551.07) and the 60-day notice rule for rent increases above 10% (Ord. 3525-2024)
- Columbus [source-of-income protections](/blog/columbus-source-of-income-protections-landlord-guide/)

Lancaster has not adopted its own version of these, based on a review of the city’s [codified ordinances](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/lancaster/latest/overview) (current through December 31, 2025). Confirm with the City of Lancaster before relying on that, since ordinances passed in 2026 may not yet be codified.

### State and federal rules that do apply

Ohio’s landlord-tenant chapter governs: habitability duties under ORC 5321.04, 24-hour entry notice that “is presumed to be a reasonable notice in the absence of evidence to the contrary” under ORC 5321.04(A)(8), and security deposit returns within 30 days under [ORC 5321.16](/blog/security-deposit-rules-ohio-landlord/). Ohio’s fair housing statute, ORC 4112.02(H), and the federal Fair Housing Act apply as well.

County rental registration works differently here. [ORC 5323.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5323.02) requires owners to file contact information with the county auditor, but the auditor’s notice duty applies to counties with more than 200,000 residents. Fairfield County counted [158,921 people in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_County,_Ohio), so ask the Fairfield County Auditor whether it collects rental filings.

Given the age of the housing stock, the [federal lead-based paint disclosure rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards) (40 CFR 745 Subpart F and 24 CFR 35 Subpart A) will apply to most Lancaster rentals. For homes built before 1978, landlords must provide the EPA pamphlet, disclose known lead hazards, attach the lead warning statement to the lease, and keep signed records for three years. Paid repair work that disturbs painted surfaces in those homes also falls under EPA’s Renovation, Repair and Painting rule.

### Evictions and short-term rentals

Lancaster evictions are filed in Fairfield County Municipal Court. The court’s [eviction packet](https://www.fcmcourt.org/docs/document_selector/upload/Civil%20Forms/Eviction_packet.pdf) (revised October 24, 2024) lists a $140 filing fee, schedules trial about 21 days after filing, and requires LLCs and trusts to use an attorney. Franklin County’s fees are different, so Columbus eviction cost figures won’t match Lancaster’s; see the [Ohio eviction cost breakdown](/blog/eviction-cost-ohio/) for how the process works.

If you are considering short-term rental use, watch city council. A proposed short-term rental ordinance (Temporary Ordinance 31-25) would have required permits with a [$150 initial fee and $75 annual renewal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/8003458/ohio/fairfield-county/lancaster/lancaster-city-council/lancaster-planner-presents-shortterm-rental-code-owners-and-tax-officials-urge-clearer-collection-rules); council [tabled it on August 25, 2025](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/8003505/ohio/fairfield-county/lancaster/lancaster-city-council/council-passes-resolution-and-several-ordinances-summary-of-actions-aug-25-2025). Check its current status before buying for that purpose.

## Bottom Line for Investors

Lancaster is an affordable entry market with real cash-flow potential, best suited to investors who are comfortable with older housing stock and price rents to the local income base. It won’t deliver Dublin-style appreciation. It does offer a stable, lower-mobility renter population, a hospital-anchored job base, and purchase prices tens of thousands of dollars below the Columbus median.

The owners who do well here buy the house, not the spreadsheet: inspect the plumbing and electrical before closing, hold a larger reserve, and set rent where a median Lancaster household can qualify. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the average rent in Lancaster, Ohio in 2026?**

September 2026 listing data puts the typical Lancaster rent between about $1,100 and $1,292 across all unit types, depending on the source (Redfin, Zumper, RentCafe).

**Is Lancaster, Ohio a good place to buy a rental property?**

It can suit cash-flow investors: prices sit below the Columbus median while three-bedroom rents reach $1,560 to $1,780, though older housing needs more maintenance capital.

**Does Lancaster, Ohio have a rental registration requirement?**

As of October 2026, Lancaster has no city rental registry, and the Columbus registry does not apply there. Confirm with the city and the Fairfield County Auditor.

**Do Columbus source-of-income and Pay to Stay rules apply in Lancaster?**

No. Those are Columbus City Code provisions that apply only inside Columbus city limits, and Lancaster has not adopted equivalents based on its codified ordinances through 2025.

**How much does an eviction filing cost in Lancaster?**

Fairfield County Municipal Court’s eviction packet lists a $140 filing fee, with trial set about 21 days after filing; attorney fees are separate.
