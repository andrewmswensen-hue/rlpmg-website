TL;DR

To switch property managers in Ohio without losing rent, read your agreement’s termination terms, time the handoff so it lands right after a rent cycle closes, and request a closing accounting, tenant ledgers, and a per-tenant security deposit schedule. Ohio brokers must hold rents and deposits in a separate trust account and account to owners at least quarterly (ORC 4735.18; OAC 1301:5-5-11, as of November 2026), so those records should already exist.

Key Takeaways

- Ohio law sets no notice period for ending a management agreement; your own agreement controls the notice, fees, and expiration date.
- The cleanest cutover lands right after the outgoing manager collects and disburses a month’s rent.
- Your rents, reserves, and tenants’ deposits sit in the outgoing broker’s trust account and have to be accounted for and remitted (ORC 4735.18).
- Security deposits should move with a per-tenant schedule both managers sign, including any interest owed under ORC 5321.16.

## Before You Give Notice: Read These Five Clauses in Your Management Agreement

Ohio law doesn’t set a termination notice period for property management agreements. [ORC 4735.18](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4735.18) requires written agency agreements to carry a definite expiration date (division (A)(28)), and the rest comes from the contract you signed. Pull it out before you call anyone, and find these five things:

1. **Termination notice.** How many days, whether notice has to be written, and whether it can only take effect on a certain day of the month.
2. **Termination or early-exit fees.** Some agreements charge a flat fee, a set number of months of management fees, or a fee per occupied unit.
3. **Leasing clawbacks and “tenant placement” language.** Some contracts say the manager is owed a fee, or keeps a claim, on tenants it placed. Read this before you assume a current lease moves with you for free.
4. **The expiration date and renewal terms.** An agreement that auto-renews for a year may have a short window to opt out.
5. **Who holds the deposits, and any non-solicitation clause** that limits contact with tenants or vendors.

If a clause is unclear, ask the outgoing manager in writing how they read it. An Ohio attorney can tell you how it applies to you. RLPM’s guide to [what to look for in a management agreement](/blog/what-to-look-for-in-a-property-management-agreement/) covers the clauses worth comparing on the way in, too.

## How Do You Time a Property Management Switch Around the 1st?

Rent moves in a monthly cycle: tenants pay, the manager posts payments, pays bills, and disburses the balance to you. Owners lose track of a month’s rent when a switch lands in the middle of that cycle. The best time to switch managers is right after a rent cycle closes. The safer plan lets the outgoing manager finish one full cycle while the incoming manager gets ready to collect the next.

Here’s an example timeline for a January 1, 2027 effective date, assuming a 30-day notice clause:

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| By November 30, 2026 | Written termination notice delivered |
| December 1 | Written records and accounting request sent |
| December 15 | Tenant change-of-manager letters mailed |
| Early January | Outgoing manager posts January rent, pays final bills, disburses |
| February 1, 2027 | Incoming manager collects its first rent |

The alternative is a clean cutover where tenants pay the new manager for January. That works, but tenants need the new payment instructions by mid-December, and anyone on autopay with the old company has to switch in time. Expect a few January payments to land in the wrong place either way.

Watch three money items at cutover: how the final month’s management fee is prorated, which vendor invoices are still open, and when your reserve balance is released. RLPM’s post on [how owner disbursements are timed](/blog/pm-owner-rent-disbursement/) shows how that last payment usually works.

> **The best time to switch managers is right after a rent cycle closes.**

## What Ohio Law Requires Your Current Manager to Hand Over

### Trust account money: rents, deposits, and reserves

An Ohio broker who manages property has to keep a separate trust account in an Ohio depository, used only for rents, security deposits, escrow funds, and other money it holds for others. Any interest goes to owners pro rata ([ORC 4735.18(A)(27)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4735.18)). The Division of Real Estate’s rule adds detail: a ledger for each owner with a running balance, tenant deposits held in that account (unless the lease and the agreement say otherwise) and credited to each tenant, and an accounting to the owner at least quarterly ([OAC 1301:5-5-11](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-1301:5-5-11)).

Failing to account for or remit money that belongs to others within a reasonable time is grounds for discipline under ORC 4735.18(A)(5). Your reserves are your money. Ask for the closing balance in writing. Ohio law treats your rents and your tenants’ deposits as trust money. Ask for the ledger before you ask for the keys.

> **Ohio law treats your rents and your tenants’ deposits as trust money. Ask for the ledger before you ask for the keys.**

### Records to request

Brokers have to keep complete transaction records for three years (ORC 4735.18(A)(24)), so the documents you need exist. Send one written request that lists everything:

- Signed leases and addenda for each unit
- Tenant ledgers for the last 12 months
- A security deposit schedule by tenant
- Move-in inspection reports and photos, plus the most recent inspection
- Open and completed work orders, and any pending turn scopes or bids
- Vendor list with W-9s and insurance certificates
- Keys, fobs, garage remotes, and lockbox codes
- Utility account details and HOA contacts
- Appliance model and serial numbers, and any warranties
- Rental applications on file, and any pending notices or legal actions

### If the handoff stalls

Follow up in writing and set a date. If a licensed broker still won’t account for your money, you can file a complaint with the Ohio Division of Real Estate and Professional Licensing. A complaint starts a disciplinary review; it won’t move your money by itself, so keep your own copies of every request.

## Security Deposits: Moving Them Without Creating Liability

Deposits are where a switch can create a problem you don’t see until a tenant moves out. Under [ORC 5321.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16), a landlord has 30 days after termination and delivery of possession to return the deposit with an itemized list of deductions. If the tenant has been in possession six months or more, the landlord owes 5% interest per year on the amount above the greater of $50 or one month’s rent, computed and paid annually.

Three steps keep the records straight when deposits change hands:

1. Get a per-tenant deposit schedule (tenant, unit, amount, date received, interest accrued) and have both managers sign off on it.
2. Have the incoming manager deposit the funds into its own trust account and credit each tenant.
3. Tell each tenant in writing who holds the deposit now.

Ask the outgoing manager to reconcile accrued interest on any deposit large enough to earn it, and ask your attorney who owes interest for the period before the transfer. RLPM’s guide to [Ohio security deposit rules](/blog/security-deposit-rules-ohio-landlord/) walks through the return process in more detail.

## Telling Tenants (and the City) Who Manages the Property Now

Existing leases stay in effect when you change managers. Tenants don’t sign new ones. They do need to know who to pay and who to call.

[ORC 5321.18](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.18) requires a written lease to include the owner’s and agent’s names and addresses, and a landlord who fails to provide them loses the right to certain tenant notices under ORC 5321.07. Inside Columbus, the housing code also requires owners to give tenants, in writing, the name, address, and phone number of the owner or agent responsible for maintenance, and a P.O. box doesn’t count ([Columbus City Code (CCC) 4551.01](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4551REOWOC)).

A good change-of-manager letter covers four things: the new manager’s name and contact details, how and where to pay starting on a specific date, where the security deposit is held, and how to submit maintenance requests, including the emergency line.

Then update the registries. In Columbus, a change of manager is also a change to your rental registry. You have 30 days.

- **Columbus Rental Registry.** You have 30 calendar days to update your registration when required information changes, including the local operator ([CCC Chapter 4515](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4515REREREPR), Ord. 0923-2026). Owners of 10 or more units need a local operator within 100 miles, and a licensed Ohio property manager can fill that role. You file through your own account on the Franklin County Auditor’s rental registration page. See RLPM’s [Columbus rental registry update](/blog/columbus-rental-property-registry-update/) for the filing steps.
- **County rental registration.** Ohio’s statewide filing with the county auditor has to be updated within 60 days of a change, and a managing agent may file on the owner’s behalf ([ORC 5323.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5323.02)).

> **In Columbus, a change of manager is also a change to your rental registry. You have 30 days.**

## Portfolio-Specific Problems: Scattered Sites, Mixed Lease Dates, and Vacancies Mid-Transition

One house can switch with a phone call and a box of keys. A portfolio has moving parts on different clocks.

**Stagger or single cutover.** With 8 to 20 doors, a single effective date is easier to reconcile. Staggering by property makes sense only when some agreements end on different dates, or when one property is mid-eviction.

**Vacant units mid-turn.** Decide who finishes the turn. Get the turn scope, vendor bids, and any photos before the outgoing manager stops working on it.

**Renewals inside the window.** A lease expiring within 60 days of the switch needs a clear owner. In Columbus, an increase above 10% on a lease entered or renewed after January 31, 2025 requires at least 60 days’ written notice under [CCC 4551.071](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4551REOWOC) (Ord. 3525-2024). If neither manager owns the renewal, that notice date can slip past.

**Pending evictions.** Find out which attorney holds the case and whether the filing names you, your LLC, or the manager. An LLC owner needs an attorney in Franklin County Municipal Court.

Write the 14-door transition plan down, item by item, with a name next to each one. On an example 14-door portfolio with three vacancies and two renewals pending, the work might divide like this:

| Item | Outgoing manager | Incoming manager |
|---|---|---|
| December rent and disbursement | Collects and disburses | None |
| Three vacant units | Hands over scope, bids, photos | Finishes turns and lists |
| Two renewals due in February | Sends nothing new | Sends renewal offers and any 60-day notice |
| Security deposits (11 occupied units) | Transfers funds and schedule | Deposits to trust, notifies tenants |
| Columbus registry and county filing | None | Owner updates within 30 days (city) and 60 days (county) |

> **Write the 14-door transition plan down, item by item, with a name next to each one.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much notice do I need to give my property manager?**

Whatever your management agreement says. Ohio law doesn’t set a notice period, so read the termination clause and any fees before you send written notice.

**What happens to my tenants’ security deposits when I switch property managers?**

They should move from the outgoing broker’s trust account to the new one with a per-tenant schedule. Ohio’s rule requires deposits to be credited to each tenant.

**Do tenants have to sign new leases when the property manager changes?**

Generally no. Existing leases stay in effect, but tenants need written notice of the new agent and new payment instructions.

**Can my old property manager keep my reserve funds?**

No. Ohio brokers must account for and remit money that belongs to others within a reasonable time under ORC 4735.18.

**Do I need to update the Columbus Rental Registry when I change managers?**

Yes, within 30 days if the local operator or other required information changes.

**What records should I request from my old property manager?**

Leases, tenant ledgers, the deposit schedule, inspection reports, work orders, vendor details, keys and codes, and any pending legal actions.
