TL;DR

A year-end statement from your property manager should show gross rent collected, management and leasing-related fees, and repairs, with capital improvements listed separately. Those figures map to IRS Schedule E: rent on Line 3, management fees on Line 11, repairs on Line 14. For tax year 2026, a manager files Form 1099-MISC for rents of $2,000 or more paid over to you. Mortgage interest, insurance, and property taxes you paid directly won’t appear on it.

Key Takeaways

- Report gross rent on Schedule E Line 3, not the net deposits that hit your bank account. Fees and repairs are deducted separately.
- Starting with payments made in 2026, the Form 1099-MISC rent threshold rose from $600 to $2,000 under Public Law 119-21.
- A $9,000 roof replacement is generally depreciated over 27.5 years, not deducted as a repair in the year you pay it.

## What Should a Year-End Statement Include?

Think of it as twelve monthly owner statements rolled into one, sorted by tax category instead of by date (see [how to read your monthly owner statement](/blog/property-management-monthly-statement/)). At minimum, look for these items:

- **Gross rent collected**, before fees come out.
- **Management fees.** At RLPM’s Standard plan ($137 per month), that is $1,644 per unit for the year.
- **Other manager fees**, such as a $250 lease renewal, a $300 onboarding fee, or the $125 annual accounting and technology fee.
- **Repairs**, itemized by vendor and date.
- **Capital improvements**, listed separately.
- **Reserve activity**: what you contributed and what was drawn.

Two items trip up owners. Reserve contributions are your own money held in trust, so they aren’t deductible (the plumber paid from that reserve is). And security deposits you hold and expect to return are [not income under IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527). A deposit you keep for damage, or one applied as the last month’s rent, generally is.

Pub 527 also treats advance rent as income when received, so January rent collected December 29 lands on this year’s statement.

## How Does It Map to Schedule E?

Rental income and expenses go on [Schedule E (Form 1040), Part I](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040se.pdf). Line numbers below are from the current (2025) form; confirm them on the 2026 version.

| Statement Item | Schedule E Line | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rent | 3 (Rents received) | Should reconcile to your 1099-MISC |
| Listing and advertising costs | 5 (Advertising) | If billed |
| Cleaning, lawn care, routine upkeep | 7 (Cleaning and maintenance) | Recurring work |
| Monthly management fees | 11 (Management fees) | Deductible in the year paid |
| Repairs | 14 (Repairs) | Improvements excluded |
| Utilities paid during vacancy | 17 (Utilities) | Between tenants |
| Capital improvements | 18 (Depreciation) | 27.5 years (Form 4562) |
| Renewal, onboarding, tech fees | CPA decides (often 8, 11, or 19) | Lease costs may be spread over the lease term |

What won’t be on the statement: anything you paid yourself. RLPM, for example, can pay property bills for owners but not mortgages, insurance, property taxes, or HOA dues. Mortgage interest (Line 12) comes from your lender’s Form 1098; insurance (Line 9) and property taxes (Line 16) from your own records. See [what a property manager can pay on your behalf](/blog/property-manager-pay-bills-landlord/).

> **Your year-end statement is the bridge between your property manager and your CPA. If it’s unclear, your taxes will be too.**

One more mapping trap: report gross rent, not the net deposits you received. If $21,600 in rent came in and $3,100 went to fees and repairs, Line 3 shows $21,600. Entering $18,500 and then deducting the fees again understates income and double-counts the expenses.

## Red Flags to Check Before Your CPA Does

### Numbers that don’t reconcile

Add up your twelve monthly statements. They should match the year-end version, and gross rent should match your 1099-MISC. A gap usually means a late-December transaction, a reversed charge, or a deposit posted to the wrong category.

### Vague line items

“Maintenance: $4,812” with no invoices behind it gives your CPA nothing to classify. Each charge should trace to a vendor invoice you can open in your owner portal.

### Improvements mixed in with repairs

Patching a leaking roof section is a repair. Replacing the roof is a restoration, which the IRS treats as an improvement to be depreciated. Lumping both into “repairs” overstates your deduction, the kind of error that tends to surface in an audit.

The [IRS tangible property regulations](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations) offer three safe harbors that let you deduct some costs that might otherwise be capitalized:

- **De minimis:** up to $2,500 per invoice or item (without audited financial statements), by annual election.
- **Routine maintenance:** recurring work you expect to do more than once in a building’s first 10 years in service.
- **Small taxpayer:** for buildings with an unadjusted basis of $1 million or less, if annual repairs and improvements stay under the lesser of 2% of that basis or $10,000.

### No 1099-MISC

According to the [2026 instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec), a property manager must use Form 1099-MISC to report rent paid over to the owner. For payments made after December 31, 2025, the threshold is $2,000 (up from $600) under [Public Law 119-21](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1), indexed for inflation starting in 2027. Corporate owners generally won’t receive one. Because January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday, expect your 2026 form by February 1, 2027.

> **Management fees, maintenance, insurance, and most operating expenses are deductible. Your manager’s statement should make that easy to see.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the 1099 threshold for rent collected by a property manager in 2026?**

$2,000, for payments made after December 31, 2025, per the IRS 2026 Form 1099-MISC instructions. The previous threshold was $600.

**Is a security deposit taxable income?**

Not if you expect to return it. It becomes income if you keep it for damage or apply it as the last month’s rent, per IRS Publication 527.

**Can I deduct a new roof or HVAC system as a repair?**

Generally no. Replacements like these are improvements, depreciated over 27.5 years for residential rental property unless a safe harbor applies.
