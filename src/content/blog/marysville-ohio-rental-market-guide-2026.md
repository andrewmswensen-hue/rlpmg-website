TL;DR

The Marysville Ohio rental market is anchored by Honda’s Marysville Auto Plant (about 4,400 employees) and one of Ohio’s fastest-growing counties. Typical rent runs about $1,400 to $1,510 a month (RentCafe, Zillow, fall 2026), roughly level with Columbus, while single-family houses list around $1,725 to $2,700. Rents dipped slightly over the past year as new apartments opened. Columbus-only landlord rules do not apply here.

Key Takeaways

- Marysville grew from 25,617 residents in April 2020 to an estimated 30,190 in July 2025, a 17.9% jump, per the U.S. Census Bureau.
- Honda cancelled its Ohio-built 0 Series EVs in March 2026, but the Marysville Auto Plant continues building gas and hybrid Accords and the Acura Integra.
- Zillow’s typical Marysville rent was $1,510 in August 2026 (down 0.8% year over year), almost identical to Columbus’s $1,495 citywide average.
- An illustrative single-family rental at $2,100 a month against Zillow’s $368,459 typical home value produces a gross yield near 6.8% before expenses.
- Columbus’s rental registry, Pay to Stay ordinance, and rent-increase notice rule stop at Columbus city limits; Marysville landlords follow Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law.

Most suburban rental markets around Columbus run on commuters. Marysville runs on a single auto plant and the employers clustered along US-33, and that changes what an out-of-state or long-term investor needs to underwrite.

## Why Do Renters Choose Marysville and Union County?

### Honda is the anchor

Honda began building cars at the [Marysville Auto Plant](https://manufacturing.honda.com/ohio/marysville-ohio/marysville-auto-plant/) in November 1982. Honda’s [2026 North America manufacturing factbook](https://hondanews.com/en-US/releases/digital-factbook-north-america-manufacturing) lists the plant at about 4,400 employees, 220,000 vehicles of annual capacity, and $5.5 billion of cumulative investment. It builds the Accord, Accord hybrid, and Acura Integra.

Two other Honda plants sit in other counties: the East Liberty Auto Plant (CR-V and Acura MDX, about 2,600 employees) in Logan County and the Anna Engine Plant (about 2,900 employees) in Shelby County. Both widen the pool of Honda workers who might live in Marysville, but only the Marysville plant is a Union County address.

The 2026 story investors should know: on March 12, 2026, Honda [cancelled the 0 Series Saloon, 0 Series SUV, and Acura RSX](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/honda-cancels-0-series-evs-ohio/814632/), the EVs it had planned to build in Ohio, and said it will continue producing gasoline and hybrid vehicles at Marysville and East Liberty. The multibillion-dollar Honda and LG Energy Solution battery plant (L-H Battery Company) is in Jeffersonville, Fayette County, south of Columbus. It is not a Union County employer, and its future product mix is now under discussion.

> **Honda’s presence gives Marysville something most suburban markets lack: a major employer anchoring year-round rental demand.**

### A deeper employer bench than most people assume

Honda is the headline, but Union County-Marysville Economic Development’s [largest-employers list](https://www.growunioncountyohio.com/largest-employers) also includes Memorial Health (about 1,181 employees), The Scotts Miracle-Gro Company, headquartered in Marysville (about 1,000), Marysville Exempted Village School District (683), and the Transportation Research Center (517). The list is undated, so treat those counts as approximate.

The [33 Smart Mobility Corridor](https://www.econdev.dublinohiousa.gov/ohio-cuts-ribbon-on-worlds-most-connected-highway-the-33-smart-mobility-corridor/), a 35-mile stretch of fiber-connected US-33 opened in September 2021, runs from Dublin through Marysville to TRC in East Liberty. For renters, that highway is the practical link to Dublin and northwest Columbus jobs. Marysville residents averaged a 24.1-minute commute in the Census Bureau’s 2020 to 2024 estimates.

### Growth, schools, and newer housing

Union County reached an estimated 73,446 residents in July 2025, up 17.0% since April 2020, according to [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/unioncountyohio/PST045225). [Newsweek’s analysis of Census data](https://www.newsweek.com/population-map-ohio-counties-growing-shrinking-census-data-2016908) ranked it first among Ohio counties for growth from 2020 to 2023. The city of Marysville grew even faster, reaching 30,190 residents (up 17.9%) per [QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/marysvillecityohio,unioncountyohio/PST045224).

Most of the city is served by Marysville Exempted Village School District. Much of the housing that renters tour sits in subdivisions built in the last two decades, such as Mill Valley, rather than in older rental stock. Uptown Marysville, the historic downtown along Fifth Street, provides the walkable core, and [city parks](https://www.marysvilleohio.org/181/City-Parks) including Eljer Park and Schwartzkopf Park serve the neighborhoods around them.

Marysville’s median household income was $92,619 in the Census Bureau’s 2020 to 2024 estimates; Union County’s was $112,322.

### How Marysville compares with Columbus

Zillow’s typical Marysville rent ($1,510, August 2026) sits almost exactly on Columbus’s citywide average of $1,495 for the same month. Marysville’s typical home value, though, is $368,459 per Zillow, up 2.0% over the past year. On the average unit, investors pay a suburban price for a city-level rent. Single-family houses are where Marysville rents pull ahead.

The FMR column matters if you plan to accept Housing Choice Vouchers. Marysville’s ZIP-level payment benchmarks run below market for most house rentals, so a voucher tenant in a $2,300 three-bedroom would need a local housing authority payment standard well above the $1,710 FMR. See [what changed for Section 8 landlords in 2026](/blog/section-8-changes-2026-columbus-landlords/) before you set a policy.

> **Marysville’s typical rent matches Columbus, but its typical home costs more. The single-family segment is where the math works.**

## Vacancy and Lease Timing

No public source publishes a reliable, current vacancy rate for Marysville alone. The data does show direction. Both Zillow and RentCafe recorded year-over-year rent declines in 2026, and that lines up with new apartment supply: the 240-unit [VC Center redevelopment](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/union-county/marysville-shopping-center-to-be-redeveloped-as-mixed-use-apartment-complex/) of the former Marysville Plaza on West Fifth Street, and the planned [Foundry 33](https://www.marysvillejt.com/2025/12/11/foundry-33-will-first-apartment-complex-marysville-east/) on Watkins Road (502 townhomes and apartments by Rockford Homes, construction timing still uncertain as of December 2025).

For a single-family owner, new Class A apartments are a pricing ceiling on smaller units, not direct competition for a three-bedroom house with a yard in Marysville Exempted Village schools. Price a two-bedroom condo against those concessions. Price a house against other houses.

Seasonality follows the broader Columbus pattern: spring and summer bring the most applicants, and November and December listings tend to sit longer. Plant hiring and job transfers soften that curve somewhat but do not erase it. If a lease ends in winter, offer a [renewal term](/services/marketing-and-leasing/) that rolls the next expiration into spring. RLPM’s guide on [how long it takes to rent a property in Columbus](/blog/how-long-to-rent-property-columbus-2026/) covers timing strategy in more depth.

For current managed-portfolio benchmarks, RLPM publishes a [live KPI scorecard](/key-performance-indicators/). As of July 2026 it showed a median of 11 days on market and 91% occupancy across the Columbus-area portfolio. Those are metro figures, not Marysville-only numbers.

## What Investors Should Know Before Buying

### Run the yield math with real numbers

Here is an illustrative example, not a projection. Take a three-bedroom house renting at $2,100 a month (the middle of current Homes.com listings) and a purchase price at Zillow’s typical home value of $368,459.

| Illustrative Line Item | Annual Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross rent ($2,100 x 12) | $25,200 |
| Gross yield on $368,459 | 6.8% |
| Less RLPM Standard plan ($137 x 12) and $125 annual accounting fee | $1,769 |
| Recommended cash reserve (6 months’ rent) | $12,600 (one-time) |

Taxes, insurance, maintenance, and vacancy still come out of that gross figure. A two-bedroom at $1,450 on the same price yields about 4.7% gross, which is thin for a leveraged purchase.

### Property taxes are resetting

Union County’s six-year reappraisal was due for tax year 2025 (its last was in 2019, per the [Union County Auditor](https://www.unioncountyohio.gov/real-estate-assessments)), and the county announced a [27.14% tentative increase in property values](https://www.unioncountydailydigital.com/articles/union-county-announces-27-14-tentative-increase-in-property-values-for-2025/) for 2025. Bills paid in 2026 reflect the new values. Tax rates also vary by district: a house in Marysville Exempted Village schools, one in Fairbanks Local, and one in the Union County portion of Dublin City Schools each carry different millage. Pull the parcel’s actual bill from the [Auditor’s tax rate tables](https://www.unioncountyohio.gov/union-county-tax-rates) rather than using a county average. Marysville also levies a [1.5% municipal income tax](https://www.marysvilleohio.org/143/Income-Tax), including on net profit filers; ask a CPA whether your rental income is subject to it.

### Newer stock, lower near-term maintenance

Subdivision homes from the 2000s and 2010s tend to need less capital work in the first five years than a 1920s house in German Village or an older Clintonville rental. The trade-off is HOA rules: many Marysville subdivisions have associations, and some restrict leasing. Read the declaration before you make an offer. RLPM manages rentals inside HOAs where leasing is permitted, and its [Standard and Premium plans](/pricing/) include HOA/COA support.

### Weigh the demand drivers honestly

Amazon Web Services won approval in March 2025 for a $1 billion, two-building data center campus at the [Marysville Innovation Park](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-gets-go-ahead-for-data-center-campus-in-marysville-ohio/) on Industrial Parkway, expected to finish in 2027. Jerome Township has fielded more data center proposals and [tightened its rules in March 2026](https://www.marysvillejt.com/2026/03/19/jerome-clamps-data-centers/). Data centers employ relatively few people once running. Intel’s Licking County fab is across the metro and years from production. Underwrite Honda, healthcare, and Dublin-corridor jobs, and treat everything else as upside.

> **A $1 billion data center brings tax revenue and construction crews. It does not bring thousands of permanent renters.**

Concentration risk is real, too. A market leaning on one plant moves with that plant’s production schedule, and Honda’s March 2026 EV reversal shows how fast plans change. A 6-month reserve keeps a slow quarter from becoming a forced sale.

## Rental Compliance Considerations

As of October 2026, Marysville landlords operate under Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law, [Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321). Key provisions for Union County owners:

- **Security deposits (ORC 5321.16):** return within 30 days after termination and delivery of possession, with interest owed on the portion over the greater of $50 or one month’s rent after six months. Details in RLPM’s [Ohio security deposit guide](/blog/security-deposit-rules-ohio-landlord/).
- **Entry notice (ORC 5321.04(A)(8)):** 24 hours “is presumed to be a reasonable notice in the absence of evidence to the contrary.”
- **Evictions:** Union County cases are filed in [Marysville Municipal Court](https://marysvilleohio.org/DocumentCenter/View/5904/Instructions-for-Eviction-Cases), not Franklin County Municipal Court, so filing fees and timelines differ from Columbus.
- **Fair housing:** federal Fair Housing Act and Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112 protections apply statewide.

What does not apply: Columbus’s new rental registry (Chapter 4515, with registration opening October 1, 2026), its Pay to Stay ordinance (CCC 4551.07), its 60-day notice rule for rent increases above 10% (Ord. 3525-2024), and its source-of-income protections. Each of those is a Columbus city ordinance that stops at the city limits. Watch the edges, though: parts of Union County sit inside the city of Dublin and other municipalities with their own codes, so confirm which jurisdiction a parcel falls in.

Marysville runs a [property maintenance and code enforcement program](https://www.marysvilleohio.org/441/Property-Maintenance-Code-Enforcement) that applies to rental and owner-occupied homes alike.

This section is informational, not legal advice. Consult an Ohio attorney about your specific property.

## Bottom Line for Investors

Marysville is an employment-anchored market with a growth trajectory few Ohio counties can match. Rents on the average unit track Columbus while home prices run higher, which pushes the better returns toward single-family houses in established subdivisions and away from small apartments competing with new construction. Honda’s pivot away from Ohio-built EVs did not shut down the Accord line, and the county’s population keeps climbing.

The work is in the details: parcel-level tax bills after the 2025 reappraisal, HOA leasing rules, and pricing against softening apartment rents. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the average rent in Marysville, Ohio in 2026?**

Zillow put typical Marysville rent at $1,510 in August 2026, and RentCafe reported $1,398 in September 2026. Single-family houses list higher, mostly between $1,725 and $2,700.

**Is Marysville, Ohio a good place to buy a rental property?**

It can be, particularly for single-family homes, thanks to Honda-anchored employment and 17% population growth since 2020. Yields on smaller units are thinner because home prices run above Columbus averages.

**Did Honda’s EV cancellation hurt the Marysville rental market?**

Honda cancelled its Ohio-built 0 Series EVs in March 2026 but continues building gas and hybrid vehicles at Marysville. Rents softened slightly over the past year, which lines up more closely with new apartment supply than with plant changes.

**Is the Honda battery plant in Marysville?**

No. The Honda and LG Energy Solution battery plant is in Jeffersonville, Fayette County, south of Columbus.

**Does Marysville have a rental registration requirement like Columbus?**

Columbus’s registry under Chapter 4515 applies only inside Columbus city limits. Confirm current local requirements with Marysville’s code enforcement office before leasing.

**Which school district serves Marysville rentals?**

Most of the city is in Marysville Exempted Village School District, while other parts of Union County fall in Fairbanks Local, North Union Local, or Dublin City Schools.
