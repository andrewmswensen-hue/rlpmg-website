TL;DR

In Fairfield County, Lancaster City Council passed its replacement subdivision regulations 10-0 on October 12 and took up a new impact fee section for public sites and open space. Canal Winchester’s council discussed projected 8% annual water and sewer increases at an October 5 work session. On the east side, a Reynoldsburg committee sent Council an ordinance adding short-term rentals to the city’s hotel tax. Lancaster, Fairfield County, and Grandview Heights items are in the radar section.

Key Takeaways

- Lancaster’s new subdivision regulations passed October 12. New lot splits and developments in Lancaster now follow the replacement chapters.
- Lancaster is also adding an impact fee section for public sites and open space, which would affect the cost of new residential construction.
- Canal Winchester is planning water and sewer rate increases of about 8% a year. Owners who pay utilities on [Canal Winchester rentals](/property-management-canal-winchester/) should expect higher bills starting January 2027.
- Reynoldsburg wants short-term rentals to collect the city’s hotel tax. Long-term leases aren’t affected.

## Fairfield County and the Southeast: Lancaster and Canal Winchester

### Lancaster: Replacement Subdivision Regulations Pass

***Status: Passed (unanimous vote). The new chapters replace Lancaster’s old subdivision rules.***

Lancaster City Council passed Temporary Ordinance 30-26 on third reading on October 12, 2026, by a 10-0 vote. It repeals and replaces Title One of the Planning and Zoning Code, the subdivision regulations in Chapters 1105 through 1171. The ordinance had its first reading September 14 and its second September 28.

If you own existing rentals in Lancaster, nothing about how you operate changes. If you’re thinking about splitting a lot, building on a vacant parcel, or a small subdivision, read the new chapters before you hire an engineer, since requirements for lot layout, streets, and utilities may differ from the old code.

[View Source Document](https://www.lancasterohio.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_10122026-1718)

### Lancaster: New Impact Fee for Public Sites and Open Space

***Status: Introduced, public hearings pending. Two more readings to go under the committee’s three-reading schedule.***

The same night, Council took up Temporary Ordinance 31-26 on first reading. It amends Part Thirteen of the Building Code, Chapter 1345 (Impact Fees), to add a new Section 1345.21 titled “Public Sites and Open Space.” The minutes record a 10-0 roll call on the item.

Cities charge impact fees on new construction to pay for the public facilities growth requires. A new open-space fee would add to the cost of building new homes in Lancaster, which matters to investors buying new construction or building rentals. The title doesn’t state the fee amount, so watch for it at the second and third readings.

[View Source Document](https://www.lancasterohio.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_10122026-1718)

### Canal Winchester: Council Weighs Yearly Water and Sewer Increases

***Status: Under discussion (no vote scheduled). Council introduced the rate ordinances later in the month.***

At an October 5, 2026 work session, Canal Winchester City Council discussed water and sewer revenue with staff. One council member asked staff to show what the 8% increases projected over the next four years would do to revenue and expenses if usage stayed flat. The city adjusts water and sewer user fees each January 1.

If tenants pay the water and sewer bill in your Canal Winchester rentals, expect it to come up at renewal. If you pay utilities yourself (common in small multifamily with a shared meter), build higher rates into your 2027 budget now.

*Update, November 2026: Canal Winchester introduced Ordinances 26-021 and 26-022 on October 19, setting 2027 rates of $7.70 per 1,000 gallons for water and $7.15 per 1,000 gallons for sewer. See the October 16 to 31 roundup.*

[View Source Document](https://www.canalwinchesterohio.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_10192026-801) (the October 19 packet includes the October 5 work session transcript).

## East Side: Reynoldsburg

### Reynoldsburg: Hotel Tax Would Cover Short-Term Rentals

***Status: Recommended for approval (awaiting Council vote). The committee forwarded it for a first reading.***

At its October 12, 2026 meeting, Reynoldsburg City Council’s Development, Parks and Recreation Committee forwarded an ordinance amending Chapter 192 (Hotel Tax) of the Codified Ordinances. City Attorney Shook explained that Ohio expanded the definition of a hotel for bed tax purposes some years ago, and the city needs to update its code to match. The update would include short-term rentals in the tax.

The ordinance also changes where the money goes. Development Director Meyer proposed sending half of hotel tax revenue to a community improvement corporation for revitalizing Old Reynoldsburg, which he estimated at about $300,000 to $350,000 a year. Mayor Begeny said 25% would stay with the visitors bureau, as required to keep collection authority, and the remaining 25% would go to Council for public art and neighborhood entryways.

Long-term rentals aren’t touched by this. If you run or are considering a short-term rental in Reynoldsburg, plan on registering for and collecting the city’s lodging tax once the ordinance passes, and price your nightly rates with that in mind.
