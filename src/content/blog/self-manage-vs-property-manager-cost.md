## What does professional management cost in Columbus?

Start with the side that has a price tag on it. RL Property Management prices management as a flat dollar amount per unit rather than a percentage of rent: Passive at $117 a month, Standard at $137, Premium at $184. Scope and the maintenance approval limit separate them ($1,500 per item on Passive, $750 on Standard, $350 on Premium). Standard adds HOA and COA support; Premium adds pre-listing support and use of your own vendors.

Leasing is $0 on all three plans, which matters more than it sounds.

| Cost line (one unit, $1,400/mo rent) | Passive | Standard | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management fee (12 months) | $1,404 | $1,644 | $2,208 |
| Annual accounting & technology fee | $125 | $125 | $125 |
| Leasing fee (tenant placement) | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Lease renewal fee (renewal years only) | $250 | $250 | $250 |
| Annual cost, turnover year (no renewal fee) | $1,529 | $1,769 | $2,333 |

Two costs sit outside that table: a one-time $300 onboarding fee when a unit comes on vacant, and repairs as they happen (in-house maintenance at $84 per hour plus a $15 trip charge and materials, or vendor cost plus a 15% project management fee capped at $450 per project, not applied to turns). Five to nine units earn a 10% discount per unit.

### Flat rate versus percentage: which one is cheaper depends on the year

Most Columbus management companies price as a percentage of collected rent plus a separate leasing fee. [Evernest Columbus publishes](https://www.columbuspropertymanagement.co/pricing) an 8% management fee, tenant placement at 50% of one month’s rent ($500 minimum), a $250 renewal fee, and a $149 annual property condition report. Run those on the same $1,400 rental and the answer flips with [turnover](/blog/rental-property-turnover-cost-columbus/): about $2,193 in a year the unit turns, roughly $1,743 in a renewal year. Set those against the Standard column above. Neither model is a trick. They are two bets about how often your unit changes hands, and scopes differ between companies, so compare what each plan includes before comparing what each charges.

> **Flat pricing wins in the years a unit turns. Percentage pricing wins in a long, quiet tenancy. Know which year you are in.**

## What does self-managing actually cost?

The self-managing column starts at $0 in fees. The rest is real money arriving in forms most owners do not track.

### Your time, priced honestly

There is no reliable public benchmark for hours per unit per month, so treat any single number with suspicion, including this one. Build it from the work instead: showings during a vacancy, application screening, posting rent and chasing the late payer, finding a vendor who will show up, reconciling the books, answering the Sunday email, plus the annual round of renewal negotiation, tax recordkeeping, and now a city registration filing. RL Property Management’s [self-management calculator](/self-management-calculator/) defaults to five hours per door per month at $50 an hour, an estimate rather than a measured figure. Call it 60 hours and $3,000 a year on one door, then substitute your own rate and your own honest hour count.

### Vacancy days you did not have to spend

Vacancy is the largest swing factor here and the easiest to measure. RL Property Management’s [2026 Columbus Single-Family Rental Market Report](/2026-columbus-market-report/) puts well-priced single-family rentals in desirable submarkets at 14 to 21 days to lease once rent-ready. Watch those two qualifiers. *Well-priced* and *once rent-ready* are variables a self-managing owner controls, and both get left on the table by pricing off a neighbor’s asking rent rather than recent signed leases, or by listing a unit that shows as almost finished.

Two occupancy figures belong side by side, since they usually get quoted apart. The market report puts occupancy above 95% for well-managed Columbus properties against 93.9% nationally (Arbor, Q2 2026), which is a market observation. RL Property Management’s own [live KPI scorecard](/key-performance-indicators/) showed 91% occupancy, a median 11 days on market, and 12 days to turn as of July 2026. Different questions, different numbers; the live page is the honest place to check either. On a $1,400 rental each vacant day costs about $46, and a listing on four platforms instead of 45 is where those days come from.

### Retail pricing on repairs, and repairs you did not see coming

Without a standing vendor relationship you pay whatever the plumber quotes on a Tuesday in February, and you wait in the same queue as homeowners. Timing costs more than pricing, though. Quarterly inspections catch the slow supply-line drip and the resident who has quietly moved a third adult in, and detectors get checked on that cycle and replaced at ten years or older (manufacturer and NFPA 72 guidance, not an Ohio requirement). Nothing stops a self-managing owner from doing all of it. Doing it four times a year, in writing, is what slips.

### Compliance exposure, where small mistakes get expensive

Ohio landlord-tenant law is specific, and the penalties attach to the specifics. Two worth knowing cold:

**Security deposits.** Under [ORC 5321.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16), deductions must be itemized in a written notice delivered to the tenant with any amount due “within thirty days after termination of the rental agreement and delivery of possession.” Miss it, and the statute says the tenant “may recover the property and money due him, together with damages in an amount equal to the amount wrongfully withheld, and reasonable attorneys fees.” The withheld amount comes back twice, plus the tenant’s legal bill. The same section requires 5% annual interest on any deposit above the greater of $50 or one month’s rent once the resident passes six months in possession. A two-month deposit carries that obligation. A one-month deposit does not.

**Entry notice.** [ORC 5321.04(A)(8)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.04) requires a landlord to “give the tenant reasonable notice of the landlord’s intent to enter and enter only at reasonable times,” and adds that “twenty-four hours is presumed to be a reasonable notice in the absence of evidence to the contrary.” That trailing clause matters: the presumption can be rebutted in both directions. Division (B) gives a tenant whose landlord violates the provision four remedies: actual damages, injunctive relief, reasonable attorney’s fees, or termination of the rental agreement.

Fair Housing sits in the same category: screening criteria applied inconsistently across applicants creates exposure a $137 monthly fee looks small against. *Informational only, not legal advice, as of September 2026. Ohio law changes, and specific situations call for a qualified attorney.*

Then the part no statement holds. Water heaters fail at 11 p.m. Self-managing means you are the call center.

> **About 3.5% of RL Property Management rentals escalate to eviction, litigation, or a significant dispute. Screening is where that number gets decided.**

## The Columbus rental registry adds a new job to your list

This one is new enough that most owners have not priced it. Columbus City Council passed [Ordinance 0923-2026](https://www.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/3/city-council/documents/city-bulletins/2026/bulletin20260425.pdf) in April 2026 by an 8-1 vote, creating Columbus City Code Chapter 4515 and a citywide rental registry. Initial registration runs **October 1 through December 31, 2026**, effective for the 2027 calendar year. The dollar cost is modest. The obligation is not.

- **$15 per unit annually**, set by ordinance 1713-2026 and frozen for at least three years. Mid-year additions pro-rated.
- **No exemption for single-family homes, duplexes, condos, or small owners.** CCC 4515.04 lists four: vacant building registry properties, hotel and short-term rental permit properties, dormitories, and facilities licensed under Chapter 4561. Nothing else.
- **No phase-in** by portfolio size, zip code, or date. Owners file in the same three-month window.
- **Updates within 30 calendar days** of any change to the required information, plus annual renewal.
- **Late fees** of 10% of annual fees up to 120 days late, rising to 20% at 120 days or more.
- **Civil penalties** under CCC 4515.995 of $2.00 per dwelling unit per day for failing to comply with a Notice of Violation by the date specified.
- **A Preventative Education Inspection on a three-year cycle** (CCC 4515.07), run by Building and Zoning Services and covered by the fee. Scope is the exterior, common areas, and shared mechanical systems, not unit interiors unless the tenant or owner asks.

For a self-managing owner with three Columbus houses that is $45 a year. The money is not the burden. The burden is a filing you have to remember, a 30-day update clock each time a phone number or mailing address changes, and an inspection somebody has to be present for.

One provision changes the math for owners who live somewhere else. Chapter 4515 requires owners of **ten or more dwelling units** to designate a Local Operator: an individual with charge, care, or control of the building whose primary residence is within 100 miles of Columbus. Below ten units, no Local Operator is required. A Columbus-area property manager satisfies it. Own eleven doors from Denver? Price that line in.

> **Registration opens October 1 and closes December 31, 2026. There is no exemption for single-family homes and no phase-in by portfolio size.**

Two open questions, stated as questions rather than answers. The fee ordinance caps fees at $1,500 *per complex*, but the text does not address scattered-site single-family portfolios, and the plain reading is that 30 houses pay 30 separate $15 fees uncapped. Ask Building and Zoning Services. Second, as of September 1, 2026 no registration portal or program page exists on columbus.gov and no rules have been promulgated under CCC 4515.01, though the city did fund the mechanism with a $399,000 contract modification for the BuildingBlocks platform. Whether it is live on October 1 is worth watching.

One more wrinkle: this is a second filing, not a replacement. [ORC 5323.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5323.02) already requires rental owners to file contact and parcel information with the county auditor and update it within 60 days of a change. It also lets a designated managing agent file on the owner’s behalf.

## What does one eviction cost in Franklin County?

Eviction is the cost self-managing owners overestimate in drama and underestimate in cash. Franklin County Municipal Court raised its civil filing fees effective **August 1, 2026**. The current [clerk’s cost schedule](https://www.fcmcclerk.com/documents/civil/civilcost.pdf) lists $149.00 for a one-cause filing (possession only) and $186.00 for two causes (possession plus money damages), each covering up to three defendants or addresses.

| Line item | Possession only | Possession + money |
|---|---|---|
| FCMC filing fee (effective 8/1/2026) | $149 | $186 |
| Local attorney cost | $100 | $100 |
| RLPM management fee for the process | $199 | $199 |
| Client cost, all in (recovery pursued from the resident) | $448 | $485 |

Roughly three steps, about six weeks, using local counsel. The hard costs are not the [expensive part.](/blog/eviction-cost-ohio/) Six weeks of a non-paying resident on a $1,400 unit is about $1,930 in rent before the turn, and a self-managing owner absorbs the filing, the hearing, the set-out, and the paperwork risk of a defective notice. Columbus also has a Pay to Stay provision (CCC 4551.06, tender of payment by third parties) barring refusal of a tender of past-due rent plus reasonable late fees based on who is paying it.

## The ROI math most owners skip

Most self-managing owners compare the management fee against $0 and stop. The more useful comparison is the fee against the outcomes it changes.

**Vacancy.** Cut one week on a $1,400 rental and you have recovered about $322. Worth being straight about: annual management runs $1,404 to $2,208, so a saved week covers a fifth of it. Vacancy carries part of this argument, not all of it. RL Property Management lists on 45 or more platforms including Zillow and Apartments.com, and reimburses the vacant-unit management fee past 60 days, which puts it on the same side of the calendar as the owner.

**Preventive catches.** A quarterly inspection that finds a failing wax ring or a leaking shutoff turns a ceiling replacement into an $84 hour of labor. That saving appears as a line item nowhere.

**Screening.** The minimums: income at three times monthly rent or better, no violent criminal convictions, no evictions in five years, no serious or recurring credit issues, income verified by pay stubs or bank statements. Roughly 3.5% of rentals escalate to eviction, litigation, or a significant dispute. Pet damage coverage runs up to $4,000 starting in 2026, funded by a resident-paid pet administration fee.

## When self-managing fits, and when it stops fitting

Plenty of Columbus owners self-manage well, and the case for it is stronger than management companies tend to admit. If you own one or two units within a 20-minute drive, have flexible daytime hours, know Ohio landlord-tenant law well enough to quote the deposit deadline from memory, and have a plumber and an HVAC tech who pick up, self-managing is likely the better financial decision. Some owners also like the work, and that preference is a legitimate input rather than a rationalization. On a paid-off duplex with a five-year resident, a management fee buys little you are not already getting.

The situations where it stops working are also recognizable:

- **Three or more units.** The coordination load stops scaling linearly around the third door.
- **Distance.** Anything past a comfortable same-day drive turns a 20-minute problem into a weekend. At ten or more Columbus units, the Local Operator requirement makes proximity a compliance question too.
- **A demanding job or a busy season at home.** The unit does not care that you are in a quarter-close or a hospital waiting room.
- **The hours have quietly passed the fee.** Track one honest month. If your time at your own rate exceeds $137, you have your answer.
- **Compliance is slipping.** A late deposit itemization, an entry without notice, or screening applied unevenly is the signal to get help before it becomes a filing.

The most common pattern is not a decision at all. Owners drift into self-managing because the first tenancy was easy, keep going through a turnover and a repair dispute and a bad winter, and run the numbers after something expensive happens. Running them first is cheaper.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is property management worth it on a single rental?**

It depends on your vacancy rate and your hourly value. Standard-plan management on a $1,400 Columbus rental runs $1,769 a year including the accounting fee. No single saved week of vacancy covers that, so the case rests on the combination: faster lease-ups, in-house repair pricing, screening, and compliance.

**How much does a property manager cost in Columbus, Ohio?**

RL Property Management charges a flat $117 to $184 per unit per month by plan, with a $0 leasing fee. Percentage-based companies commonly charge a percentage of monthly rent plus a separate tenant placement fee.

**Do I have to register my rental with the City of Columbus?**

If the property sits inside Columbus city limits and does not fall under one of the four exemptions in CCC 4515.04, yes. Initial registration runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, at $15 per unit annually. Single-family homes are not exempt.

**What does an eviction cost in Franklin County in 2026?**

The court filing fee is $149 for possession only or $186 with money damages, effective August 1, 2026. Through RL Property Management the all-in client cost is about $448, with recovery from the resident pursued where possible.

**How long does it take to lease a Columbus rental?**

Well-priced single-family rentals in desirable Columbus submarkets lease in 14 to 21 days once rent-ready, per RL Property Management’s 2026 market report, and its live KPI scorecard showed a median 11 days on market in July 2026. The company plans around a four to six week average and does not promise a timeline, because quality placement outperforms speed over a long hold.
