TL;DR

Starting October 1, 2026, RL Property Management passes the cost of professional listing photography (vendor cost plus a standard markup, typically $250 to $450) to property owners when new photos are needed. Existing photos keep working at no charge. New photos are usually only needed after a turn with major cosmetic updates. Owners keep the photos and can request copies anytime. The change keeps RLPM’s flat-rate, $0-leasing-fee pricing model transparent instead of folding photography costs into a hidden markup.

Key Takeaways

- **Effective October 1, 2026, new professional photography is billed at vendor cost plus RLPM’s standard markup, typically $250 – $450 depending on property size.**
- Existing photos are grandfathered in. If RLPM already has professional photos of a property, nothing changes.
- New photos are typically only needed after a turn involving major cosmetic work, such as new paint or flooring.
- Every photo set belongs to the property owner and is available on request, at no extra charge.
- RLPM still charges $0 in leasing fees. This change keeps photography costs visible instead of folding them into a higher flat rate.

## What’s Changing on October 1, 2026

For years, RL Property Management has absorbed the cost of professional photography for every vacant listing that needed it. Starting October 1, 2026, that changes. When a property needs new professional photos, the cost gets billed at the vendor’s rate plus a standard markup, typically landing between $250 and $450 depending on the size of the property.

This isn’t a new cost stacked on top of the [flat monthly management rate](/columbus-property-management/#pricing). It’s a specific cost, tied to a specific service, billed only when that service is actually needed. Properties that already have usable professional photos on file aren’t affected. Those photos stay in rotation at no charge for as long as they accurately show the property.

## Why Photos Are Worth Paying For

Photos aren’t a formality on a rental listing. Zillow’s own guidance for landlords lists viewing photos among the top factors renters weigh: [extremely or very important to 60% of renters](https://www.zillow.com/learn/what-to-look-for-in-apartment/), with weak or sparse photo sets generating fewer inquiries. [Zillow recommends at least 10 photos per listing](https://www.zillow.com/rentals-network/how-to-write-a-rental-ad/) for exactly that reason. PlanOmatic (one of the photography companies that we at RL partner with) [agrees](https://www.planomatic.com/blog/how-hiring-a-professional-real-estate-photographer-will-save-you-money).

Run the math on a typical $1,500-a-month Columbus rental. A listing that sits an extra week because the photos looked flat or dark costs an owner roughly $350 in lost rent, before factoring in the management cost credit RLPM issues once a property passes 60 days vacant (see current [days-on-market figures on the live KPI scorecard](/key-performance-indicators/)). A $250 to $450 photography investment that shaves even a few days off the time to lease pays for itself.

> **Good photos aren’t decoration. They’re the reason a qualified renter stops scrolling and books a showing.**

## What This Means for Your Property

A few specifics matter here. If professional photos already exist for a property, this change has no effect. Those photos stay in the listing rotation, with no retroactive charge for approving, taking, or continuing to use them.

New photography is typically needed in one of two situations: a property is coming under management for the first time and doesn’t have photos on file, or a property goes through a turn that changes how it looks (new flooring, fresh paint, updated fixtures) enough that the old photos no longer reflect what a renter would actually see. A stained carpet replaced with new hardwood, for example, deserves a new photo before the unit goes back on the market.

Every set of photos belongs to the property owner, not RLPM. They’re available on request at no additional charge, whether that’s for a future for-sale listing, an insurance file, or simply a record of the property’s condition.

> **The photos belong to the owner, not RLPM. That doesn’t change on October 1.**

## Why Transparency Beats a Hidden Markup

There’s a simpler way this could have gone: quietly raise the flat monthly management cost across all three plans and keep photography folded in as before. Every owner, whether their listing needed new photos or not, would have paid a little more every month to cover it.

That approach was rejected on purpose. RLPM’s pricing already stands out in the Columbus market for what it doesn’t charge (no leasing fee on any of the three plans, a policy few local competitors match). Bundling a variable, situational cost like photography into the base rate works against that same principle: pay for what a property actually needs, and see the number on an invoice instead of guessing what’s baked into an additional cost.

> **A $250 to $450 line item is easier to trust than a rate hike nobody can point to a reason for.**

A line item billed only when a shoot actually happens is easier to explain, easier to audit, and easier to trust than a rate increase with no clear cause attached to it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When does this take effect?**

October 1, 2026. It applies only to new professional photography ordered on or after that date.

**Will I be charged for photos RLPM already has of my property?**

No. Existing photos stay in use at no cost as long as they still represent the property accurately.

**How much does new photography cost?**

Vendor cost plus RLPM’s standard markup, typically $250 to $450 depending on property size.

**Do I get to keep the photos?**

Yes. Photos belong to the property owner and are available on request at no extra charge.

**Why change this now?**

To keep pricing visible. A photography line item that only appears when a shoot happens is easier to explain than an extra cost folded into a higher flat rate.
