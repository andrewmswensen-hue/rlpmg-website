## Why Thorough Resident Screening Matters

A **single bad resident** can turn a profitable rental into a financial drain. Evictions alone cost U.S. landlords an average of **$3,500 or more**, factoring in lost rent, legal fees, and property damage. Even if an eviction isn’t necessary, a poorly screened resident can lead to **late payments, excessive wear and tear, and frequent turnover**, all of which **erode your bottom line**.

**That’s why consistent, thorough screening is the best way to protect your investment. Here’s why:**

- **Stable, On-Time Rent Payments**, Residents with **strong credit and verifiable income** are far more likely to **pay on time every month**, ensuring steady cash flow.
- **Reduced Property Damage**, Careless or irresponsible residents can **cause thousands in preventable repairs**. A thorough screening process helps filter out those with a history of **property neglect**.
- **Lower Turnover Costs**, A well-qualified resident is **more likely to stay long-term**, saving you money on re-listing, marketing, and vacancy periods.
- **Fewer Legal Headaches**, Screening helps **reduce the risk of lease violations, unauthorized occupants, and eviction disputes**, all costly, time-consuming issues.

Columbus’s rental market is **growing and becoming more competitive**. With rising demand, landlords must be **more selective than ever** to secure **responsible, long-term residents** who treat their homes (and your investment) with respect.

A clear, **consistent screening process ensures you attract the best applicants**, minimizing risks and maximizing your rental income. We’ve boiled it down to *five key steps*that any Columbus landlord can use to find, screen, and place great residents for their property.

## Step 1, Pre-Screening Your Prospects

Before a prospective resident fills out an application, smart landlords **filter out unqualified candidates upfront**, saving time, reducing risk, and ensuring compliance with **Fair Housing laws**.

## Set Clear Rental Criteria

Start by defining **non-negotiable requirements** and making them clear in your listing and initial conversations. Common criteria include:

- **Income-to-rent ratio**, Require **at least 3x the monthly rent** in gross income.
- **Credit score minimum**, Generally, **650+** is a good benchmark.
- **Rental history**, No **evictions in the past 3 years** and positive references.
- **Pet policy**, If applicable, clarify breed restrictions, pet fees, or deposits.
- **Occupancy limits**, Follow **local safety codes** to avoid legal risks.

## Pre-Screen Before the Application

Once you start receiving inquiries, **respond efficiently** with a few key questions to filter out **non-qualified applicants** before they apply. You can do this via email, phone, or an online form.

**Example Pre-Screening Questions:**

- What is your **monthly income** (before taxes)?
- What is your **approximate credit score**?
- Have you ever been **evicted or broken a lease**?
- Do you have **pets? If so, what breed and size?**
- How many people will be living in the rental?
- When are you looking to move in?

### Fair Housing Compliance Tip

Avoid discriminatory language or policies. Statements like **“No kids”** or **“Ideal for young professionals”** could **violate Fair Housing laws**. Instead, focus on **objective, financial qualifications** that apply **equally to all applicants**. This **pre-screening process weeds out unqualified prospects early**, reducing **time wasted on bad applications** and ensuring a smooth screening experience.

## Step 2, The Rental Application Process

Once a prospect passes pre-screening, the next step is collecting a **comprehensive rental application**. This ensures you have **all the necessary information** to properly evaluate their qualifications.

## What to Include in a Rental Application

- **Personal Information**, Full **legal name, date of birth, Social Security Number (SSN), and contact details** for identity verification and screening purposes.
- **Employment & Income Verification**, Require **current job details, employer contact information, and proof of income** (pay stubs, tax returns, or bank statements for self-employed applicants). A stable income **at least 3x the monthly rent** is a strong indicator of financial responsibility.
- **Rental History**, Request **at least two previous landlords’ contact information** and ask about **payment history, lease violations, or evictions**.
- **Background & Credit Check Consent**, Ohio law requires **written authorization** before running any credit or background reports. Include a **clear consent statement** in the application.
- **Emergency Contact Information**, Useful in case of **urgent maintenance issues or non-payment scenarios**.

### Ohio Laws and Fair Screening Practices

Unlike some states, **Ohio does not have a statewide cap on application fees**. However, fees should **only cover actual screening costs** (e.g., credit/background checks). Charging excessive fees may deter good applicants and lead to legal scrutiny.

### Fair Housing Compliance Tip

**Require every adult (18+) who will live in the rental to submit a separate application**, no exceptions. This ensures **fair and consistent screening** for all prospective residents.

## Step 3, Conducting Background and Credit Checks

A thorough background and credit check helps landlords assess financial responsibility and identify potential risks before approving a resident. These reports provide insights into an applicant’s credit history, eviction record, and criminal background, ensuring a well-informed decision.

## How to Run a Credit Report Legally

Landlords must obtain **written consent** from applicants before conducting any background or credit check. Once consent is secured, use a **reputable tenant screening service** such as:

- TransUnion SmartMove
- Experian RentBureau
- Equifax Resident Screening
- RentPrep or other landlord-focused services

These platforms provide a detailed **credit report, rental payment history, and eviction records**, helping landlords make data-driven decisions.

## Credit Score Benchmarks

A credit score offers insight into an applicant’s financial responsibility. While it should not be the sole deciding factor, it plays a key role in the screening process.

- **700+**, Low-risk applicant, strong financial reliability.
- **650-699**, Moderate risk; consider employment stability and rental history.
- **Below 600**, High risk; may indicate frequent late payments or financial instability. In such cases, evaluate rental history and income carefully before making a decision.

## Evaluating Evictions and Rental History

Eviction records are a major red flag, as past evictions often indicate future risk. Generally:

- **Evictions within the past 3 years** suggest a high-risk applicant.
- If an eviction is older than 3 years, consider **circumstances, financial recovery, and rental history since the eviction**.

### Ohio Law Compliance Tip

Under Ohio law, landlords must **obtain written consent** before conducting any background or credit checks. Failure to do so can lead to legal liability. Ensure all applicants sign a **screening authorization form** as part of their rental application.

## Interpreting Credit and Eviction History

A credit report provides valuable insight, but landlords must look beyond the score alone. Not all debt is equal, and a **holistic review** of financial behavior is key.

- **Medical debt vs. unpaid rent**, Medical debt does not necessarily indicate financial irresponsibility, whereas **unpaid rent or utility bills** signal a higher risk.
- **Recent eviction (past 3 years)**, A strong red flag. Residents with recent evictions often struggle with financial stability and lease compliance.
- **Multiple late payments on past rentals**, Consistent late payments can indicate difficulty in managing financial obligations.
- **Stable credit with minor issues**, If an applicant has a fair credit score but **strong rental history and income**, they may still be a reliable resident.

Instead of focusing solely on credit scores, landlords should **look for patterns** in financial behavior and rental history. A well-documented **rental payment record** can sometimes outweigh minor credit concerns.

## Verifying Income and Employment

Confirming an applicant’s **income stability** is critical to ensuring their ability to pay rent consistently. Landlords should verify income using multiple sources to **reduce fraud risk**.

### Required Income Documentation:

- **Pay stubs**, Request at least **two recent pay stubs** for employed applicants.
- **Tax returns and bank statements**, For self-employed or gig workers, request **the last two years of tax returns**or **bank statements showing consistent deposits**.
- **Employer verification**, Call HR departments or supervisors to confirm **employment status and income consistency**. If needed, LinkedIn or company websites can help verify job legitimacy.

### Income-to-Rent Ratio Rule

A general rule of thumb is that a resident’s **gross monthly income should be at least three times the rent**. This ensures they have sufficient income to cover rent while managing other financial responsibilities.

### Special Considerations for Freelancers and Gig Workers

For self-employed individuals, ask for **an average of their past 12 months’ earnings** to ensure income stability. Reviewing **bank deposits and profit trends** can help assess their financial reliability.

## Step 4, Calling References and Previous Landlords

Speaking with a past landlord is one of the most valuable steps in resident screening. A credit report can reveal financial history, but a landlord reference **provides firsthand insight into a person’s behavior as a resident**.

## Key Questions to Ask a Previous Landlord:

- **Did they pay rent on time?** Late or partial payments could indicate financial instability.
- **Did they maintain the property well?** Excessive damage beyond normal wear and tear is a red flag.
- **Were there any lease violations?** Ask about noise complaints, unauthorized occupants, or property damage.
- **Would you rent to them again?** If the answer is “no,” dig deeper to understand why.

## Spotting Red Flags

Not all landlords provide accurate references. Some **may give a glowing review just to get rid of a problem resident**. To avoid misinformation:

- **Compare the reference to the rental application.** Inconsistent details could indicate dishonesty.
- **Ask specific questions.** A hesitant or vague response may suggest the landlord is withholding details.
- **Look for repeat issues.** A single late payment may not be a dealbreaker, but a pattern of missed rent or complaints is concerning.

Checking references takes **a few extra minutes** but can save **thousands in potential damages and eviction costs**. A strong landlord reference is a **powerful sign of a responsible resident**, while an evasive or negative reference should give landlords pause.

## Step 5, Making a Decision and Offering the Lease

Once all screening data is collected, landlords must **compare applicants against their written criteria** to make an informed and fair decision.

## Evaluating Applicants Fairly

- **Review all screening factors together**, Credit score, income, rental history, and references should be considered holistically.
- **Stick to objective criteria**, Consistency is key to avoiding **Fair Housing violations**. Do not make exceptions based on personal assumptions.
- **Weigh red flags carefully**, If an applicant has one concern (e.g., lower credit), assess whether other strengths (strong income, good rental history) balance it out.

## Approving and Sending a Lease Offer

- **Act quickly**, Strong applicants secure rentals fast. Once approved, send a lease offer promptly to avoid losing them to another property.
- **Require a security deposit and first month’s rent** to secure the unit.
- **Provide clear lease terms and expectations** to ensure transparency.

## Rejecting an Applicant

- **Follow Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) guidelines**, If rejecting an applicant due to credit history, landlords must provide an **Adverse Action Notice** explaining the decision and including contact information for the credit reporting agency.
- **Keep documentation**, Maintain records of the screening process to defend against any potential discrimination claims.

A fair, consistent approach to screening helps landlords secure **responsible, long-term residents** while avoiding legal risks.

## Ohio-Specific Laws and Fair Screening Practices

Ohio landlords must comply with **federal, state, and local Fair Housing laws** when screening residents. Key legal considerations include:

- **Protected Classes**, In addition to federal protections (race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, and national origin), **Ohio law includes military status and ancestry** as protected classes.
- **Consistent Screening**, Every applicant must be evaluated using the same criteria. Selective enforcement can lead to **discrimination claims**.
- **Application Fee Transparency**, Landlords must disclose any **application fees upfront** and ensure fees only reflect actual screening costs.
- **Data Security**, Sensitive applicant information (SSNs, credit reports) must be stored securely and disposed of properly.

By following these guidelines, landlords can ensure **a fair and legally compliant screening process** while protecting their investments.
