## Set Clear Expectations from Day One

Late rent issues often begin with one simple problem: unclear expectations. If tenants don’t know exactly what’s expected, or what happens when they fall behind, they’re far more likely to miss payments. That’s why the best rent collection strategy starts *before* the lease is even signed.

### Put It in Writing

Your lease agreement should clearly outline all rent-related terms. Avoid vague or legal-heavy language. Instead, use plain, straightforward wording and make the rent section easy to find.

**What to include in the lease:**

- **Monthly rent amount** and **due date**
- **Grace period** (if any) and exact cutoff time (e.g., by 11:59 PM on the 5th)
- **Late fee amount** and when it’s applied
- **Preferred payment methods** (online portal, ACH, etc.)
- **Consequences of non-payment** (including notices, eviction steps, etc.)

💡 *Pro Tip:* Use a bolded or boxed section labeled **“Rent Payment Terms”** so nothing gets overlooked.

### Define Due Dates, Grace Periods, and Penalties

In Columbus, Ohio, most leases use this structure:

- **Rent due on the 1st**
- **Grace period until the 5th**
- **Late fees begin on the 6th**

Be specific about what happens if the 5th falls on a weekend or holiday: will you push to the next business day or not? Clarity here reduces gray areas that lead to disputes.

### Communicate at Onboarding

Verbal communication reinforces what’s written in the lease. When tenants move in:

- Walk through the rent collection process during your welcome call or meeting
- Send a simple “How to Pay Rent” PDF or video link.
- Set the tone early by emphasizing the importance of on-time payments.

🎯 *Your Goal:* Make sure your tenants know exactly when, how, and where to pay rent, and what happens if they don’t.

## Offer Multiple and Convenient Payment Options

One of the simplest ways to improve on-time rent payments is to make paying rent as easy and convenient as possible. The less friction there is in the process, the more likely tenants are to pay on time, and the less time you’ll spend chasing payments.

### Online Portals vs. Manual Payments

Modern property management software like **Buildium** or **AppFolio** makes paying rent simple and fast. These systems:

- Provide **24/7 access** for tenants to submit payments
- Automatically **track payment history** for both you and your tenants.
- Send **automated reminders** when rent is due or late.

Manual payment methods like checks or money orders are outdated. They’re slow, prone to delays, and harder to track, creating unnecessary risk for both you and your tenants.

### Encourage Recurring Payments

Recurring payments (or autopay) are one of the easiest ways to ensure timely rent:

- Offer a small incentive, such as waiving a minor admin fee, for tenants who sign up for autopay.
- During onboarding, show tenants how to “set it and forget it.”

The more tenants who enroll, the more predictable your cash flow becomes.

### Avoid Cash or Personal Checks

Cash is risky (theft, loss, no paper trail), and personal checks can bounce. Many landlords in Columbus have eliminated these options to protect their income stream.

### Flexible Payment Tools

Where possible, allow tenants to pay via:

- **Bank transfer (ACH)**
- **Debit card**
- **Mobile apps** (if supported by your system)

The easier you make it to pay rent, the fewer excuses tenants have for being late.

## Communicate Proactively, Not Just When Rent Is Late

Clear, timely communication plays a major role in getting rent paid on time. Many landlords only reach out when rent is already overdue, but by then, the issue may have already snowballed. A proactive approach helps tenants stay on track and shows you run your rentals like a business.

### Send Pre-Rent Reminders

A simple, friendly reminder can make all the difference, especially in a world where people are juggling bills, work, and family.

**Send reminders:**

- **By email or text** around the 28th or 29th of each month
- Include a direct link to the payment portal.
- Keep the tone polite and professional.

Example:

 “Hi , just a quick reminder that your rent is due on the 1st. You can pay anytime using the online portal: . Let us know if you have any questions!”

### Follow Up Immediately When Rent Is Missed

Don’t wait until the grace period expires. If rent isn’t received by the 2nd or 3rd:

- Send a neutral message confirming that the payment hasn’t posted
- Offer support; sometimes it’s a simple oversight.

Example:

 “We noticed your rent payment hasn’t come through yet. If you need help accessing the portal or have any issues, let us know right away.”

### Stay Professional, Not Personal

- Use templates or scripts to take emotion out of the equation.
- Avoid judgment or speculation, stick to the facts.
- Never assume malice, just enforce the policy.

### Keep Records

Always document:

- Messages sent
- Dates of follow-up attempts
- Tenant responses (if any)

🛠 This creates a clear paper trail that’s essential if things escalate to a legal issue.

## Enforce Policies Consistently and Fairly

It’s one thing to have policies on paper; it’s another to enforce them consistently. Inconsistent enforcement not only weakens your authority but also invites ongoing payment issues. Tenants quickly learn what they can “get away with,” and even good renters will start testing boundaries if late fees or penalties feel optional.

### Why Consistency Builds Trust

- Tenants are more likely to follow the rules when they know they’ll be upheld.
- Consistent enforcement prevents accusations of favoritism or discrimination.
- It signals professionalism and keeps the landlord-tenant relationship on business terms.

### Applying Late Fees

Your lease should outline exactly:

- **When a late fee is applied** (e.g., after the 5th of the month)
- **How much it is** (fixed dollar amount or percentage of rent)
- Whether it **accrues daily** or is a one-time charge

Use your rent collection software to automate the late fee application. That way, the system, not you, enforces the rules, and tenants can’t argue favoritism.

### Document Everything

Create a reliable system to track:

- **Payment ledgers** showing rent due, rent received, and outstanding balances
- **Communication logs**: every text, email, or phone call should be recorded.
- **Legal notices**, what was sent, when, and how (email, certified mail, door posting)

🗂 If you ever have to go to court, this documentation will protect you and strengthen your case.

⚠️ *Remember:* If you enforce rules loosely, tenants will too. Being consistent isn’t harsh; it’s fair, professional, and key to long-term success.

## Know When (and How) to Escalate Non-Payment

Sometimes, even with the best systems and communication, rent still doesn’t come in. When that happens, it’s important to act fast and follow the right legal steps. Delaying action doesn’t just affect your cash flow; it also sends the wrong message to the tenant.

### Use the 3-Day Notice Promptly.

In Ohio, once the grace period has ended and rent is officially late, you can issue a **3-Day Notice to Pay or Vacate**.

**Best practices:**

- Don’t wait. Post the notice as soon as the lease allows.
- Follow your local regulations: in Franklin County, that means including specific language and attaching recent rent statements.
- RL Property Management prepares and posts these notices professionally, on time, every time.

### Work With Legal Experts or a Property Manager

Evictions are legal procedures, not DIY projects. Errors can cause delays, court rejections, or legal liability.

**A good PMC or eviction attorney will handle:**

- Filing the eviction with the county
- Attending court hearings
- Coordinating with the bailiff if a setout is required
- Ensuring proper documentation throughout the process

📌 With RLPM, the eviction process includes fixed legal fees, court filings, and even setout coordination if needed.

### Understand the True Cost of Delays

Delaying enforcement can cost thousands in:

- **Lost rent** (weeks or even months)
- **Legal fees** (attorney, court, administrative)
- **Turnover costs** if the property is left in poor condition.

Quick, decisive action isn’t about being harsh; it’s about protecting your asset and income stream.

🧭 *Bottom line:* When rent goes unpaid, every day counts. Escalate smart, escalate early, and follow procedure.
