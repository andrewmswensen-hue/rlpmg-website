TL;DR

A property manager can pay the bills that keep a rental running: repair and maintenance invoices, vendor and turn costs, and utilities while the unit sits vacant, all paid out of rent or your reserve before your net income is deposited. At RL Property Management in Columbus, four bills stay with the owner: the mortgage, property insurance, property taxes, and HOA or COA dues.

Key Takeaways

- RLPM pays property-related bills from rent or reserves, except HOA dues, mortgages, insurance, and property taxes.
- Each RLPM plan sets a per-item repair approval limit: $1,500 (Passive), $750 (Standard), or $350 (Premium). Larger repairs go to the owner first.
- Ohio law requires a brokerage managing property to hold rents and security deposits in a separate trust account (ORC 4735.18(A)(27)).
- Bills you pay directly won’t show up on your manager’s [year-end statement](/services/owner-reporting/), so bring those records to your CPA yourself.

## What Bills Can a Property Manager Pay for a Landlord?

If a bill exists because the property operates as a rental, a manager can usually pay it. At RLPM, that covers four groups (part of [what a Columbus property manager does](/blog/what-does-a-columbus-property-manager-do/)).

**Maintenance and repair invoices.** [In-house work](/services/maintenance/) runs $84 an hour plus a $15 trip charge and materials, so a two-hour faucet and disposal repair comes to $183 before parts. Outside vendors bill at their own rate plus a 15% project management fee on vendor costs up to $3,000, capped at $450 per project. A $2,000 roof patch from a vendor carries a $300 fee.

**Turn costs between residents.** Paint, cleaning, and flooring get paid the same way, with a tiered fee: 15% on turns up to $15,000, 10% from $15,001 to $25,000, and 7.5% above that.

**Utilities during vacancy.** RLPM keeps utilities on while a unit is empty, at the owner’s expense. Vendors need power and water, and heating a vacant Hilliard split-level in January costs far less than a burst pipe.

**Other property bills you approve.** Recurring or one-time bills tied to the property can be paid once you sign off.

## Can a Property Manager Pay Your Mortgage, Taxes, or Insurance?

Not at RLPM. The mortgage, property insurance premiums, property taxes, and HOA or COA dues stay with the owner, paid directly from your own accounts. Other managers draw this line differently, so ask.

Owners have practical reasons to keep these four close. Many mortgages already handle two: the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-an-escrow-or-impound-account-en-140/) explains that a lender-run escrow account collects part of each mortgage payment to pay property taxes and homeowners insurance.

The stakes on a missed payment also land squarely on the owner of record. In Franklin County, tax year 2025 bills were due [February 28 and July 20, 2026](https://treasurer.franklincountyohio.gov/About-Taxes/Collection-Dates), and under [ORC 323.121](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-323.121) late real estate taxes draw a 10% penalty (half is waived if paid within ten days). Lender, insurer, and HOA notices go to you, the owner of record.

> **A property manager can handle the day-to-day bills. The mortgage, insurance, and taxes stay with you.**

## How Bill Payment Works Month to Month

Rent comes in, approved bills go out, and the rest is deposited to you. In between, [ORC 4735.18(A)(27)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4735.18) requires a brokerage managing real property to hold rents and security deposits in a separate trust account. On timing, see [how rent disbursement works](/blog/pm-owner-rent-disbursement/).

### Approval limits decide who signs off

Your plan sets how large a repair can be before the manager calls you. On Passive ($117/month), that’s $1,500 per item. Standard ($137/month) sets it at $750, and Premium ($184/month) at $350. A $600 dishwasher replacement moves ahead without a call on Passive or Standard, but a Premium owner hears about it first. Emergencies, like a failed furnace in February, get handled right away through the 24/7 call center.

### Reserves cover the gaps

Some months, a vacancy plus a turn outruns rent. RLPM requires a maintenance reserve of $350 to $1,500 per unit, depending on plan, and recommends owners hold about six months’ rent overall. On a $1,500-a-month Westerville duplex unit, that’s $9,000. See [building a rental property reserve fund](/blog/property-reserve-fund-rental/).

> **For an owner in Denver or Dallas, a local team paying the plumber is often the most useful part of management.**

### Payments stay on the record

Each bill paid shows up in the 24/7 owner portal (invoices included) and on your monthly PDF statement: what was paid, when, and to whom. See [how to read your monthly owner statement](/blog/property-management-monthly-statement/).

One tax-season catch: mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, and HOA dues are deductible rental expenses under [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527) (ask your CPA about HOA dues), but since you pay them yourself, they won’t appear on your manager’s year-end statement. Pull those records separately. See [how to read your year-end tax statement](/blog/property-management-year-end-tax-statement/).

The cleanest setup for most owners: let the manager run the property’s bills, keep the four ownership bills on autopay or in escrow, and check the portal monthly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a property manager pay my mortgage?**

RLPM does not pay mortgages; owners pay their servicer directly. If your loan has an escrow account, your servicer typically pays property taxes and insurance from it.

**Does a property manager pay the utilities on a rental?**

RLPM pays utilities while a unit is vacant, at the owner’s expense. During a lease, the lease terms decide who pays.

**Where does the money for bills come from?**

Approved bills are paid from rent collected or from your maintenance reserve before net income is deposited to your account.

**Can a property manager pay HOA dues?**

Not at RLPM. Owners pay HOA and COA dues directly, though the Standard and Premium plans include help with HOA and COA matters.
