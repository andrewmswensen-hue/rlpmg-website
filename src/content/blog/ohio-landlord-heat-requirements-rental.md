TL;DR

Ohio law (ORC 5321.04) requires landlords to supply reasonable heat at all times unless the heat comes from equipment under the tenant’s exclusive control on the tenant’s own utility connection. Landlords must also keep any heating equipment they supply in good working order. In Columbus, the housing code (CCC 4523.05, as of November 2026) requires heat capable of keeping habitable rooms and bathrooms at 70°F when it’s 0°F outside.

Key Takeaways

- Ohio’s statute requires “reasonable heat at all times” but sets no temperature; Columbus sets 70°F at 0°F outside.
- A tenant who pays the gas bill doesn’t take over the furnace. Landlords maintain heating equipment they supply (ORC 5321.04(A)(4)).
- Columbus bans unvented space heaters and open-flame radiant space heaters in rentals.
- Five or more heating violation notices within 12 months can put a Columbus property into conditional registry status (CCC 4515.09).
- Treat a winter no-heat call as an emergency, with a stopgap plan ready before the first cold night.

## What Ohio Law Requires: “Reasonable Heat at All Times”

[Ohio Revised Code (ORC) 5321.04](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.04) lists the landlord’s duties. Division (A)(6) requires running water, reasonably heated water, and “reasonable heat at all times.” Two exceptions apply: a building the law doesn’t require to have the equipment, and heat or hot water generated by an installation in the tenant’s exclusive control and supplied by a direct utility connection.

That second exception is about who pays for the gas. Division (A)(4) handles the furnace itself: it requires landlords to keep heating fixtures and appliances they supply in good and safe working order. If you put the furnace in the house, the repair is yours even when the gas account is in the tenant’s name. Tenant-paid gas does not make the furnace the tenant’s problem. If you supplied it, you maintain it.

> **Tenant-paid gas does not make the furnace the tenant’s problem. If you supplied it, you maintain it.**

For a number, look to the city.

## How Warm Does a Columbus Rental Have to Be?

Inside Columbus city limits, [Columbus City Code (CCC) 4523.05](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4523LIVEHE) sets the standard (Ord. 2019-2021, July 26, 2021):

- Heating facilities must be properly installed, vented, and maintained, and capable of heating habitable rooms, bathrooms, and toilet rooms to at least 70°F when the outdoor temperature is 0°F.
- When the occupant controls the heat, the occupant must run it enough to keep pipes from freezing.
- When the owner, operator, or agent controls the heat, they must maintain at least 70°F whenever it’s 0°F or warmer outside.
- Unvented space heaters are prohibited, and so are open-flame radiant space heaters.
- Room heaters need clearance of 12 inches at the rear and sides and 24 inches at the top and front.
- Fireplaces not designed for coal or wood can’t burn them.

CCC 4551.01 restates the owner’s general responsibility for heating equipment.

Own rentals in Gahanna, Westerville, Dublin, or another suburb? Check that city’s property maintenance code, since the temperature and enforcement can differ from Columbus.

## What Happens When the Furnace Fails in January?

A no-heat call in freezing weather is an emergency. A cold house can turn a furnace repair into a burst pipe within a day. Your first job is getting a technician out. Your second is keeping the house warm until the repair is done.

**Stopgap heat.** Approved electric space heaters can bridge a short outage, but stay inside the CCC 4523.05 limits: no unvented or open-flame units, and keep the required clearances. If parts are days away, some owners pay for a hotel stay. That’s the owner’s call.

**Tenant remedies.** A tenant can’t stop paying on their own say-so. Under [ORC 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07), a tenant who is current on rent can give written notice, and if the landlord doesn’t fix the condition within a reasonable time or 30 days (whichever comes first), deposit rent with the court clerk. The rent escrow section doesn’t apply to a landlord with three or fewer units who gives written notice of that fact in the lease.

**City enforcement.** Columbus can issue emergency vacate orders ([CCC 4509.06](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4509ENNOPE)), and when the condition was the owner’s responsibility, the owner reimburses the city’s tenant relocation costs: up to three months of temporary housing, or three times the HUD fair market rent for a permanent move (CCC 4509.061). Housing code violations are first-degree misdemeanors, with fines up to $1,000 per offense for an individual and up to $5,000 for an organization, and each day can count as a separate offense (CCC 4509.99). In January 2025, Columbus sued the owners of a Far East Side apartment complex over [units with little or no heat](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2025-01-23/city-of-columbus-sues-owners-of-far-east-side-apartments-with-little-or-no-heat) (WOSU).

**Registry status.** Under the new rental registry, five or more violation notices for heating (CCC 4523.05) and certain other conditions within 12 months, following initial registration, can put a property of up to 100 units into conditional status ([CCC 4515.09](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4515REREREPR)). RLPM’s [Columbus rental registry update](/blog/columbus-rental-property-registry-update/) explains what conditional status costs. A three-day furnace delay in January can turn into a code case, a relocation bill, and a registry flag.

> **A three-day furnace delay in January can turn into a code case, a relocation bill, and a registry flag.**

## Prevent It: Fall Service, Filters, and a Plan for the First Cold Night

Fall is the time to head off a January outage. A short checklist covers most of it:

- **Annual furnace service** before heating season, with the report kept in the property file.
- **A filter schedule** the tenant can follow, or that your manager handles at inspections.
- **Working CO detectors** wherever the code requires them. RLPM’s guide to [smoke and CO detector requirements](/blog/smoke-co-detector-requirements-ohio-rental/) lists the Ohio and Columbus rules.
- **A thermostat minimum in the lease or move-in packet,** so tenants who travel don’t let the house drop low enough to freeze pipes.
- **Heat in vacant units.** Keep utilities on and the thermostat set during a turn. RLPM keeps utilities on during vacancies for this reason.

RLPM’s [fall maintenance checklist](/blog/fall-winter-maintenance-checklist-columbus-rental/) and its guide to [preventing frozen pipes](/blog/winter-ready-rentals-columbus-frozen-pipes/) cover the rest of the winter prep.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What temperature must a landlord keep a rental in Columbus?**

Columbus requires heating that can keep habitable rooms and bathrooms at 70°F or warmer when it’s 0°F outside (CCC 4523.05).

**Does an Ohio landlord have to fix the furnace if the tenant pays the gas bill?**

Generally yes. Landlords must keep heating equipment they supply in good and safe working order under ORC 5321.04(A)(4).

**How long does a landlord have to fix the heat in Ohio?**

Ohio uses a reasonable time based on the condition, capped at 30 days for rent escrow purposes. No heat in winter calls for an emergency response.

**Can a tenant withhold rent for no heat in Ohio?**

Not by withholding on their own. A tenant who is current on rent must give written notice and can then deposit rent with the court clerk if the landlord doesn’t fix the problem in time.

**Are space heaters allowed in Columbus rentals?**

Unvented space heaters and open-flame radiant space heaters are prohibited, and other room heaters need set clearances from walls and furniture.
