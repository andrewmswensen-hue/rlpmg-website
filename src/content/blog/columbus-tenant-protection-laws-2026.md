TL;DR

As of October 2026, Columbus landlords inside city limits must follow city ordinances on top of federal and Ohio law: source-of-income protection (CCC 4551.03), deposit installments for operators with 5+ units (4551.04), rent and deposit receipts (4551.05), third-party rent and Pay-to-Stay (4551.06 to 4551.07), 60 days’ notice for rent increases over 10%, expanded fair housing classes (2331.02), anti-retaliation rules, and a new rental registry with registration due by December 31, 2026.

Key Takeaways

- Columbus tenant protection laws apply only inside Columbus city limits; Gahanna, Worthington, and other suburbs have their own rules.
- A Section 8 voucher counts as income in Columbus, and income ratios apply to the tenant’s share of rent, not the contract rent.
- Renter’s Choice deposit installments apply to operators with 5 or more rental units, not to all Columbus rentals.
- Pay-to-Stay lets a tenant who tenders past-due rent, reasonable late fees, and court costs before judgment raise a defense to eviction.
- Initial registration for the new Columbus rental registry runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, at $15 per unit.

## What Are Columbus’s Tenant Protection Laws?

Columbus tenant protection laws are city ordinances, most of them in [Chapter 4551 of the Columbus City Code](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT45HOCO_CH4551REOWOC), that add landlord obligations beyond what the federal Fair Housing Act and Ohio’s landlord-tenant statute (ORC Chapter 5321) already require. City Council passed most of them between 2021 and 2026 under the [Housing for All Columbus](https://www.housingforallcbus.com/) agenda.

The catch is geography. A duplex in Clintonville or a house in Linden sits inside city limits, so these rules apply. A rental in Gahanna or Upper Arlington does not, even with a Columbus mailing address. Some suburbs have their own versions (Gahanna, Reynoldsburg, and Worthington each have a pay-to-stay ordinance), so check the municipality, not the ZIP code.

> **Columbus landlords operate under three layers of housing law: federal, state, and city. Missing any one of them creates exposure.**

### Columbus tenant protections at a glance

| Protection | Code section | Effective | What landlords must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of income | CCC 4551.03 (Ord. 0494-2021) | July 1, 2021 | Count vouchers and other lawful income; same terms for all |
| Renter’s Choice | CCC 4551.04 (Ord. 0495-2021) | Leases signed or renewed from July 1, 2021 | 5+ units: offer deposit up front, over 3, or over 6 months |
| Rental receipts | CCC 4551.05 (Ord. 0496-2021) | July 1, 2021 | Receipt for deposit and each rent payment |
| Third-party rent | CCC 4551.06 (Ord. 2108-2023) | Passed 2023 | Accept rent paid by others for the tenant |
| Pay-to-Stay | CCC 4551.07 (Ord. 2109-2023) | Passed July 31, 2023 | Accept a qualifying tender of past-due rent before judgment |
| Rent increase notice | Ord. 3525-2024 (Chapter 4551) | Leases after Jan. 31, 2025; penalties from July 1, 2025 | Lease must promise 60 days’ notice for increases over 10% |
| Expanded fair housing | CCC 2331.02 | In effect | Adds sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age 40+ |
| Anti-retaliation | CCC 4509.07 (Ord. 1156-2023) | Amended 2023 | No retaliation for good-faith complaints |
| Relocation cost recovery | Ord. 1987-2024 (Titles 45 and 7) | Passed July 15, 2024 | City can bill owners for emergency relocation costs |
| Rental registry | CCC Chapter 4515 (Ord. 0923-2026) | Register Oct. 1 to Dec. 31, 2026 | Register units for 2027; $15/unit |

### Does Ohio law override these ordinances?

Ohio does limit what cities can do. [ORC 5321.19](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.19), effective September 23, 2022, bars local measures that conflict with Ohio’s landlord-tenant chapter “or that regulate the rights and obligations of parties to a rental agreement that are regulated by this chapter,” and it specifically bans rent control. Housing, building, health, and safety codes are carved out.

That language is broad. Even so, as of September 2026 the Columbus ordinances remain on the books and courts are applying them. In December 2025, Ohio’s Tenth District Court of Appeals referenced the Columbus tender defense in [*Nationwide Children’s Hospital v. Harper*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10766718/nationwide-childrens-hosp-v-harper/) without addressing preemption. This review found no appellate decision striking down a Columbus tenant ordinance under ORC 5321.19 and no 2025 to 2026 state law preempting them. Treat them as enforceable.

## Can a Columbus Landlord Refuse a Section 8 Voucher?

No. Inside Columbus, refusing an applicant because the rent would be paid with a Housing Choice Voucher violates [CCC 4551.03](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=4810997&GUID=F8814ABC-2462-4E56-8228-CE21CFE2CDE3), enacted by Ordinance 0494-2021 (passed March 8, 2021, effective July 1, 2021). “Source of income” is defined in CCC 4501.378 as any lawful source that can be verified and substantiated: wages, Social Security, SSI, public assistance, child or spousal support, and housing vouchers.

It reaches beyond the yes-or-no decision. According to the [city’s Housing for All summary](https://www.housingforallcbus.com/_files/ugd/9e18fa_eb6cdca9364b422ea6a885460875b829.pdf), operators can’t set different prices, deposits, fees, or lease terms by income source, can’t try to discourage an applicant, and can’t misrepresent whether a unit is available. A violation is a first-degree misdemeanor. A “no Section 8” listing invites exactly that kind of complaint.

### How income ratios work with a voucher

You can still screen on credit, rental history, criminal history (within fair housing limits), and income, applied the same way to each applicant. The difference is the math: when a voucher covers part of the rent, the income requirement applies to the tenant’s share.

Say the home rents for $1,500, the voucher pays $1,100, and the tenant owes $400. A 3x income rule means the applicant needs $1,200 a month in income, not $4,500. RLPM applies its 3x standard this way for voucher holders inside Columbus.

> **In Columbus, a voucher is income. The rent-to-income test applies to the tenant’s share, not the contract rent.**

For voucher inspections and screening language, see the [Columbus source of income protections guide](/blog/columbus-source-of-income-protections-landlord-guide/) and the [Ohio tenant screening and fair housing guide](/blog/tenant-screening-ohio-fair-housing-2026/).

## Renter’s Choice and Rental Receipts

### Renter’s Choice: deposit installments (CCC 4551.04)

Under [Ordinance 0495-2021](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=4811012&GUID=F4A5FC25-B1BE-4FCE-B627-238571B28221), a landlord who requires a security deposit must give written notice, before the lease is signed, that the tenant can choose to pay it:

- up front, before the lease begins;
- over no fewer than 3 monthly installments; or
- over no fewer than 6 monthly installments.

The tenant picks. Installments fall on the rent due date and can be paid with rent in one transaction. On a $1,500 deposit, a tenant choosing three installments adds $500 to each of the first three rent payments.

Two details get overlooked. First, it does not cover all Columbus rentals: the ordinance states it “shall not apply to any operator with fewer than 5 rental units,” per the [city’s Renter’s Choice summary](https://www.housingforallcbus.com/renters-choice). Second, it expressly allows “alternative lawful means” of securing the lease, so a deposit alternative such as a surety product can be offered on top of the three required options. Failing to provide the notice is a fourth-degree misdemeanor.

Ohio’s return and interest rules under [ORC 5321.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16) still apply once the deposit is collected; the [Ohio security deposit rules guide](/blog/security-deposit-rules-ohio-landlord/) covers them.

### Rental receipts (CCC 4551.05)

Under Ordinance 0496-2021, landlords must provide a written receipt for the security deposit and all rent payments. Cash and other payments without automatic documentation need a receipt within four business days, according to the [city’s Rental Receipt summary](https://www.housingforallcbus.com/rental-receipt). Automatic email or portal receipts count. Violations are strict liability misdemeanors, so intent doesn’t matter.

## How Does Pay-to-Stay Work in Columbus?

[Ordinance 2109-2023](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6295962&GUID=DE3FD3D2-7AEF-48A9-B7B0-3F13428BEC0B) (CCC 4551.07) gives tenants an affirmative defense in a nonpayment eviction. Before the landlord files, the tenant can tender all past-due rent plus reasonable late fees. After filing and before judgment, the tender must also cover court costs. If the landlord refuses a qualifying tender, the tenant can raise it as a defense. Late fees above the greater of $50 or 10% of monthly rent are treated as unreasonable for tender purposes.

A companion law, [Ordinance 2108-2023](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6295961&GUID=FD010D3E-E0E6-41A1-A087-56BDB09FCE9D) (CCC 4551.06), requires landlords to accept rent paid on a tenant’s behalf by someone else, such as a rental assistance agency.

> **Pay-to-Stay doesn’t prevent evictions. It adds a procedural step that many landlords don’t know about until they’re in court.**

A Franklin County eviction for possession runs roughly $448 through RLPM (the $149 filing fee, a $100 attorney fee, and RLPM’s $199 management fee), so a tenant who pays everything owed before judgment usually leaves the owner better off than a judgment would. RLPM already pursues pay-and-stay arrangements before court when a resident can catch up. The mechanics get their own article: [Pay-to-Stay in Columbus: how it works](/blog/tenant-stops-paying-rent-ohio/#columbus-pay-to-stay-the-rule-that-changes-the-endgame). For costs, see [what an eviction costs in Ohio](/blog/eviction-cost-ohio/).

## Do Columbus Landlords Have to Give Notice Before Raising Rent?

Yes, for larger increases. [Ordinance 3525-2024](https://www.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/1/city-council/documents/city-bulletins/2024/bulletin20241214.pdf), the Rental Increase Notification law, requires any written lease entered into or renewed after January 31, 2025, to include a clause guaranteeing the tenant at least 60 days’ written notice before rent goes up by more than 10%. Penalties began July 1, 2025: a first-degree misdemeanor with a fine of up to $1,000, based on the [ordinance text circulated by the Columbus Apartment Association](https://www.caahq.com/uploads/1/2/0/1/120160565/rent_increase_notification_final_draft.pdf).

The lease must also spell out recurring charges (rent, deposit, move-in and parking fees, late fees). Income-based subsidized tenancies are excluded.

This is not rent control; Columbus doesn’t cap the increase. It regulates timing. A lease renewing March 1 with a jump from $1,400 to $1,575 (12.5%) needs written notice by roughly December 31. If your lease template predates 2025, check it against the [Columbus rental lease checklist](/blog/columbus-rental-lease-checklist/).

## Fair Housing Classes, Retaliation, and Relocation Costs

### Protected classes beyond federal and Ohio law

The federal Fair Housing Act protects seven classes. Ohio’s [ORC 4112.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4112.02) adds ancestry and military status. [CCC 2331.02](https://www.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/1/public-health/columbus_codeofordinances_2331-2.pdf) goes further, prohibiting housing discrimination based on sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, and age, which CCC 2331.01 defines as “at least forty (40) years old.” Complaints go to the [Columbus Community Relations Commission](https://www.columbus.gov/Government/Mayors-Office/City-Boards-Commissions-Committees/Community-Relations-Commission/Discrimination-Protected-Classes-in-Columbus). For federal changes, see the [2026 HUD fair housing guidance update](/blog/hud-fair-housing-guidance-2026-ohio-landlord/).

### Anti-retaliation (CCC 4509.07)

Ohio already bars retaliation under ORC 5321.02. Columbus made it a crime. [Ordinance 1156-2023](https://www.housingforallcbus.com/_files/ugd/9e18fa_8c3264f262874489912ddc94004d5b10.pdf) strengthened CCC 4509.07: raising rent, cutting services, refusing renewal, or threatening eviction in response to a tenant’s good-faith complaint about conditions, or tenants organizing, is a first-degree misdemeanor. The practical defense is documentation: if a nonrenewal follows a code complaint, have a legitimate reason on file that predates it.

### Relocation costs after emergency vacates

This one is about cost recovery, not a new tenant claim against landlords. After the city spent roughly $9 million relocating residents of Sawyer Towers and Colonial Village in 2022 and 2023, Council passed [Ordinance 1987-2024](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=6119DA14-7B29-4744-AFC0-15931746AC0A&ID=6738935) on July 15, 2024. It defines relocation assistance as an abatement cost the city can recover from the property owner when code violations, such as no heat or burst pipes, force an emergency relocation.

## New This Quarter: The Rental Registry, and How to Stay Compliant

The newest obligation arrives this fall. Columbus’s rental registry (CCC Chapter 4515, Ordinance 0923-2026, passed April 2026) opens initial registration for 2027 from October 1 through December 31, 2026. The fee is $15 per unit per year, capped at $1,500 per complex. Owners of 10 or more units need a local operator within 100 miles. Failing to fix a registry violation after notice costs $2 per unit per day. Details are in the [Columbus rental property registry explainer](/blog/columbus-rental-property-registry/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do Columbus tenant protection laws apply in Gahanna, Dublin, or Westerville?**

No. Columbus City Code applies only inside Columbus city limits, though several suburbs, including Gahanna, Reynoldsburg, and Worthington, have passed their own pay-to-stay ordinances.

**Do I have to let tenants pay the security deposit in installments?**

If you operate 5 or more rental units in Columbus, yes: you must offer in writing the choice of paying up front, over at least 3 months, or over at least 6 months (CCC 4551.04).

**What is Pay-to-Stay in Columbus?**

It is an eviction defense for tenants who tender all past-due rent and reasonable late fees before filing, or those amounts plus court costs after filing but before judgment, and are refused.

**How much notice do Columbus landlords have to give before raising rent?**

For leases signed or renewed after January 31, 2025, the lease must provide at least 60 days’ written notice for any increase of more than 10%.

**When do I have to register my Columbus rental property?**

Initial registration for calendar year 2027 runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, at $15 per unit per year.

**Has Ohio preempted Columbus’s tenant protection ordinances?**

ORC 5321.19 limits local landlord-tenant rules and bans rent control, but as of September 2026 no court has struck down the Columbus ordinances covered here, and courts continue to apply them.
