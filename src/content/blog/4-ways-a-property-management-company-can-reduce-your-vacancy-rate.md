## What does vacancy actually cost?

Everything, and then the turn. A vacant unit pays taxes, insurance, utilities, lawn care and any loan payment, and collects nothing. That is what makes vacancy different from every other line in a rental budget: the loss is total, not partial.

Put a number on it before deciding what a vacant day is worth avoiding. At the RL Property Management portfolio average rent of $1,653, one day is about $54, one week is about $380, and one month is $1,653 plus whatever the turn costs. Turnover work on a Columbus single-family rental is its own bill, covered in [what a rental property turnover actually costs](/blog/rental-property-turnover-cost-columbus/).

Some downtime between residents is normal and belongs in your projections. The current portfolio numbers give a benchmark to plan against: 20 days is the median time from listing to signed lease, 12 days is the median time to turn a unit, and portfolio occupancy runs 91 percent.

> Vacancy is the only expense line where you pay everything and collect nothing. Every other cost at least buys you something.

## Why is rent pricing the biggest lever?

Because price is the one variable that changes how many people call, and nothing else you do matters until the phone rings.

The mistake is not usually greed. It is using the wrong comparison. Owners pull comps from active listings, which are asking prices on units that have not rented. Those are a ceiling. What you want is recent signed leases on comparable units in the same submarket, which is what the unit will actually clear at.

Pricing too high is expensive in a way that hides itself. Three extra weeks of vacancy chasing $75 more per month is about $1,130 of lost rent to gain $900 over a full year, and you have to hold the resident for the whole year to break even. The long version of that math is in [how overpricing your Columbus rental creates the vacancy it was supposed to prevent](/blog/rental-pricing-vacancy-columbus/).

Pricing too low is quieter and lasts longer. The unit leases in four days, which feels like success, and the gap between your rent and the market compounds for as long as the resident stays.

## How does screening reduce vacancy?

By reducing how often you have to lease the unit at all. The most reliable way to have a low vacancy rate is to have a resident who stays.

Screening is where that gets decided. The criteria applied to every applicant, the same way:

- **Income.** Combined household income of at least three times the monthly rent, verified with pay stubs or bank statements rather than a stated figure.
- **Rental history.** Contact with the current and previous landlord, confirming on-time payment, no significant balance at move-out, and no serious damage.
- **Eviction history.** No evictions in the past five years.
- **Credit history.** No serious or recurring credit issues. Credit history correlates with future on-time payment well enough to be worth weighting, which is why it is on the list, but it is one of five factors and not a single score cutoff.
- **Criminal history.** No violent criminal convictions.

Consistency is not a nicety here. Criteria applied unevenly across applicants is the exposure that turns a leasing decision into a Fair Housing problem, which is a far more expensive outcome than a slow month. The full treatment is in [how to screen tenants in Ohio without breaking Fair Housing law](/blog/tenant-screening-ohio-fair-housing-2026/).

Across the RL Property Management portfolio, about 3.5 percent of tenancies escalate to eviction, litigation or a significant dispute, and the lease renewal rate is 72 percent. Those two numbers are largely the output of the list above.

## Does allowing pets reduce vacancy?

Usually, yes, and it is one of the few policy changes that widens the applicant pool without lowering the rent. A no-pets policy removes a large share of Columbus renters from consideration before they ever inquire, and residents with pets tend to stay longer once placed, because moving with an animal is harder.

Allowing pets without a policy is the version that goes wrong. The structure that works is a one-time pet acceptance fee at move-in plus monthly pet rent, with the animal, the breed and the terms named in the lease. Residents expect this and price it in.

The wear-and-tear objection is real and it is worth pricing rather than banning. RL Property Management covers approved-pet damage above the security deposit up to $4,000, funded by the monthly pet administration fee. Service and assistance animals are not pets under Fair Housing law, cannot be charged pet fees or pet rent, and are not covered by that program. Details on lease language are in [what to include in your lease agreement about pets](/blog/yay-or-nay-to-pets-what-to-include-in-your-lease-agreement/).

## Can your fees be keeping applicants away?

Yes, in both directions, which is the part owners find counterintuitive.

Applicants compare total monthly cost across several listings. A unit with no fees and a higher rent leases slower than the same unit with normal fees and market rent, because the applicant assumes fees are coming anyway and does not discount your rent for their absence. Some fees are expected: application fees, pet fees, late fees, a lease administration fee.

The other end is worse. Fee stacking, or fees disclosed late in the process, leaves a resident feeling worked over before they have moved in, and that resident renews at a lower rate. A fee that costs you a renewal cost you a full turn to collect a few hundred dollars.

The sweet spot is the middle: charge what the market charges, disclose all of it in the listing and the application, and price the rent honestly rather than hiding rent inside fees.

## How do lease terms affect vacancy in Central Ohio?

They decide which month you have to lease in, and in Central Ohio that is worth real money. The slow season runs from late October through the New Year. Nobody wants to move in December in Ohio, so December vacancies last longer and clear at lower rent.

Nothing requires a 12-month lease. A 9-month or a 16-month term moves the expiration into spring or summer, when the applicant pool is several times larger. Doing that once, on the first lease, is the cheapest vacancy reduction available, because it costs nothing.

Longer terms are the other option. A two-year or three-year lease removes a leasing event entirely, and residents will often take one in exchange for something: a slightly lower rate than the one-year price, or a commitment not to raise rent during the term. Price that trade deliberately. Locking rent for three years in a rising market has a cost that does not appear until year three.

## Do incentives work, and when should you use one?

They work to create urgency, and they are usually the second-best answer to a slow listing.

Common versions are waiving the application fee or offering a half or full month free with a signed 12-month lease. Two cautions come with them. The incentive is the owner's money, not the manager's, so it should be a deliberate decision rather than a default response to a quiet week. And someone has to actually credit it on the ledger, which is the kind of promise that gets made in a showing and forgotten at move-in.

The alternative we generally recommend first is lowering the asking rent until the unit leases. It sounds like the same thing, and financially it often is, but it produces a better outcome. A price the market accepts tells you the unit is now correctly priced, and the resident who signs at a market rent is more likely to renew than the resident who was bought in with a free month and then faces the real number twelve months later.

## Why renewals are decided in the first month

Because residents form a view of the landlord long before the renewal notice arrives, and the first maintenance request is where that view forms.

A request answered the same day, with a repair that actually fixes the thing, buys a renewal a year later. A request that sits for a week does the opposite, and the resident has already decided by the time you are negotiating. The current portfolio median is 6 days to close a repair, and the renewal rate is 72 percent. Those two numbers move together.

Move-in condition works the same way. A unit that shows as almost finished, with the touch-up paint still pending and one appliance not installed, starts the tenancy in a hole. ## What does a property manager change about vacancy?

Four things, concretely.

- **Listing reach and response speed.** A listing syndicated broadly and inquiries answered the same day produce more showings than a single listing checked in the evenings.
- **Pricing from signed-lease data.** Comps from leases that actually closed rather than from asking prices.
- **Turn speed.** Median 12 days to turn a unit, with the scope written immediately after possession rather than after the first weekend visit.
- **Aligned incentives on vacancy.** Management fees are credited back to the owner after 60 days of vacancy, and leasing is $0 on all three plans, so there is no placement fee that rewards a turnover.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good vacancy rate for a rental property in Columbus?**

Look at occupancy rather than a target vacancy percentage on a single unit, because one house is either occupied or not. Across the RL Property Management portfolio, occupancy runs 91 percent, the median time from listing to signed lease is 20 days, and the median turn takes 12 days.

**How long should it take to rent a house in Columbus?**

Plan around the published median of 20 days from listing to signed lease, and expect a wider spread in practice. Season, price and condition drive most of the variation. A well-priced, rent-ready unit listed in May behaves very differently from the same unit listed in December.

**How much does one vacant month cost?**

At the portfolio average rent of $1,653, a vacant month costs $1,653 in lost rent plus the turn, plus the taxes, insurance and utilities that keep running. About $54 per day is the number to hold in your head when weighing a pricing decision.

**Should I lower rent or offer a move-in incentive?**

Lower the rent first. An incentive is a one-time payment out of your pocket that leaves the rent overpriced at renewal. A price the market accepts fixes the problem permanently and tends to produce a resident who renews.

**Does allowing pets really reduce vacancy?**

It widens the applicant pool, and residents with pets tend to stay longer. Charge a one-time pet acceptance fee and monthly pet rent, name the animal in the lease, and remember that service and assistance animals are not pets and cannot be charged either one.

**What is the single most effective way to reduce vacancy?**

Keep the resident you have. A renewal costs a lease amendment. A turnover costs the vacant days, the turn work, and the leasing time. Fast maintenance, fair renewal increases, and a resident who was screened properly in the first place do more for vacancy than any marketing decision.
