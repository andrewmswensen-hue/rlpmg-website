TL;DR

You can rent a property in the off-season in Columbus. Demand dips in November and December (national rents bottom out in November) and starts rebuilding in January. Price at or slightly below current comparable listings, use a one-time move-in incentive instead of a permanent rent cut, invest in daylight listing photos, and set the lease term so it ends in spring or summer, when finding the next resident is easier.

Key Takeaways

- National median rent falls about 1.6% from its summer peak to its November low (Apartment List). The slowest stretch is November and December, not the whole winter.
- A $1,500-per-month rental loses roughly $50 in rent per vacant day, so waiting for spring often costs more than a winter concession.
- A lease signed in winter can be written to end in spring or summer, when the renter pool is larger.

## Is It Harder to Rent a Property in Winter in Columbus?

Somewhat. Fewer households move over the holidays and in cold weather. [Apartment List’s seasonality research](https://www.apartmentlist.com/research/best-time-of-year-to-rent) found that national median rent falls by about 1.6% from the summer peak to its lowest point in November, with November and December the cheapest months before prices pick up again in January.

That slow window is also shrinking. A [January 2026 Apartment List analysis](https://www.apartmentlist.com/research/the-rental-markets-peak-season-is-becoming-less-pronounced) found that over 30 percent of renter interest now happens in the first quarter. For a Columbus owner, January and February belong to the next ramp-up. The real lull is mid-November through New Year’s.

The winter market has two things going for it. Some owners hold vacant units until spring, which thins out competing listings. And the people touring a Westerville duplex on December 12 usually have a reason: a job that starts in January, a lease that ended, a house sale that closed.

> **Off-season renters tend to be more motivated. Most are moving because they have to, not because the weather is nice.**

Waiting has a price, too. As an illustration, a $1,500-a-month unit earns about $49 a day ($1,500 × 12 ÷ 365), close to Columbus’s citywide average rent of about $1,495 ([Zillow, August 2026](https://www.zillow.com/rental-manager/market-trends/columbus-oh/)). Holding it empty from mid-November to March 1 gives up roughly $5,100, plus winter utilities. For current lease-up times, see [how long it takes to rent a property in Columbus](/blog/how-long-to-rent-property-columbus-2026/) and RLPM’s [live KPI scorecard](/key-performance-indicators/).

## What Works When Leasing From November Through February

### Price for the market you’re in

The spring premium doesn’t apply in December. Price at or slightly below comparable active listings from day one instead of chasing the market down. A $1,500 unit priced $25 too high that sits an extra three weeks loses about $1,050, while the $25 discount costs $300 over a 12-month lease (illustrative figures). See [how rental pricing affects vacancy in Columbus](/blog/rental-pricing-vacancy-columbus/).

### Use a one-time incentive, not a permanent cut

A one-time concession usually beats a lower rent: the discount ends after month one, and renewal increases start from the higher base.

| Option on a $1,500/mo unit | Cost over 12 months | Base rent at renewal |
|---|---|---|
| Lower rent to $1,400 | $1,200 | $1,400 |
| Half off the first month | $750 | $1,500 |
| Cover the application fee | The fee amount, once | $1,500 |

The incentive changes the price, not the screening bar.

### Make the listing work harder

In December it’s dark by dinnertime, so after-work showings happen under porch lights and photos carry more weight. Shoot on a bright day with the lights on and the walk cleared (see [listing photos and vacancy time](/blog/rental-listing-photos-vacancy-time/)). Reach matters more when fewer people are searching: RLPM [lists on 45+ platforms](/services/marketing-and-leasing/), including Zillow and Apartments.com, and keeps utilities on so the home is warm at showings.

Fees matter more in a slow month. Managers that charge a placement fee average [70.6% of one month’s rent](https://ipropertymanagement.com/research/average-property-management-fees) nationally (about $1,055 on a $1,495 unit). ## How Do You Time a Lease So It Doesn’t End in Winter Again?

Lease length is one of the most overlooked levers an owner has. A 12-month lease signed in December ends next December, right back in the slow season.

> **A 15-month lease starting December 15 ends March 14 of the year after next, right as spring leasing picks up.**

At 18 months, it ends mid-June. For current residents, if a lease expires November 30, a 6-month renewal (December 1 through May 31) bridges to spring instead of locking in another winter expiration. See [this lease renewal strategy guide](/blog/lease-renewal-rent-increase-strategy/).

Two rules apply. Inside Columbus city limits, [Columbus Ordinance 3525-2024](https://library.municode.com/oh/columbus/codes/code_of_ordinances) (codified in City Code Chapter 4551) requires leases entered into or renewed on or after January 31, 2025, to include a clause giving at least 60 days’ written notice of any rent increase above 10%. And if you let a lease roll to month-to-month over the winter, [Ohio Revised Code 5321.17(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.17) lets either side end it with at least 30 days’ notice before the rental due date, so the resident could leave in January ([pros and cons of month-to-month leases](/blog/month-to-month-lease-pros-cons-columbus-landlords/)). This is general information as of October 2026, not legal advice; have an Ohio attorney review your lease language.

A December vacancy is a pricing and timing problem, and both are within your control. Price for today, keep the incentive one-time, and write the lease so the next turnover lands in spring.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the slowest time of year to rent a property in Columbus?**

Nationally, November and December tend to be slowest, with demand rebuilding in January. RLPM’s live KPI scorecard shows current local days on market.

**Should I wait until spring to list my vacant rental?**

In most cases, no. A $1,500 unit empty from mid-November to March gives up about $5,100, more than a typical concession.

**Can I offer a lease shorter or longer than 12 months?**

Yes. Many owners use 6-, 15-, or 18-month terms to move expirations into spring or summer.

**Do winter move-in specials attract lower-quality residents?**
