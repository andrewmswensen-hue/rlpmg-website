This roundup covers October 16 to 31, 2026, grouped by area.*

TL;DR

Canal Winchester introduced 2027 rates of $7.70 per 1,000 gallons for water and $7.15 for sewer on October 19. On October 26, Reynoldsburg gave a second reading to adding short-term rentals to its hotel tax and introduced ordinances on backyard chickens and backflow testing. Worthington City Council took up its new comprehensive plan on October 19, and Plain City’s mayor said the village is working on a property maintenance code. Six more items are in the radar section, grouped by area.

Key Takeaways

- Canal Winchester’s water and sewer usage charges would total $14.85 per 1,000 gallons in 2027 and $16.04 in 2028 under the proposed ordinances.
- Reynoldsburg’s hotel tax ordinance, which adds short-term rentals, is one reading from a final vote.
- Reynoldsburg’s chicken ordinance would require at least half an acre, cap flocks at six, and require a one-time permit. Leases can still set stricter rules.
- Worthington’s comprehensive plan supports ADUs and small multifamily. The zoning code rewrite that follows will turn it into rules.
- Plain City plans a property maintenance code, which would set upkeep standards for rental exteriors in the village.

## Southeast: Canal Winchester

### Canal Winchester: 2027 and 2028 Water and Sewer Rates Introduced

***Status: Introduced, public hearings pending. Second reading was set for November 2.***

Canal Winchester City Council gave first readings on October 19, 2026 to Ordinance 26-021 (water user fees) and Ordinance 26-022 (sanitary sewer user fees). Both take effect for the billing period that includes January 1, 2027, and both set a second step for January 1, 2028.

| Charge per 1,000 gallons | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Water (domestic and sprinkler, outside the SWAD) | $7.70 | $8.32 |
| Sanitary sewer | $7.15 | $7.72 |
| Combined usage charge | $14.85 | $16.04 |

On a unit that uses 5,000 gallons in a month, for example, usage charges would come to about $74.25 at 2027 rates and $80.20 at 2028 rates, before any fixed charges. If you pay water and sewer on a [Canal Winchester](/property-management-canal-winchester/) duplex or small building, multiply that by your unit count when you set next year’s budget. If tenants pay, expect the topic to come up at renewal.

## East Side: Reynoldsburg

### Reynoldsburg: Short-Term Rental Hotel Tax Gets Its Second Reading

***Status: Second reading completed (one step from final vote). First introduced earlier in October.***

A Reynoldsburg City Council committee forwarded the ordinance amending Chapter 192 (Hotel Tax) on October 12, and on October 26, 2026, it appeared on Council’s consent agenda for second reading. The change brings the city’s code in line with Ohio’s broader hotel definition so short-term rentals pay the bed tax, and it sends half of the revenue to a community improvement corporation for Old Reynoldsburg.

If you operate a short-term rental in Reynoldsburg, the next step is a final vote. Once it passes, expect to collect the tax on each stay, and check with the city on how to register and remit it. Long-term leases are unaffected.

### Reynoldsburg: Backyard Chickens Would Be Allowed With Limits

***Status: Introduced, public hearings pending. Council held work sessions on the idea in fall 2025.***

On October 26, Council introduced an ordinance amending Section 505.02 (Permitted and Prohibited Animals) to allow residential chicken ownership. Under the proposal, owners would need at least half an acre on a single residential parcel, fenced under the zoning rules. The ordinance caps flocks at six birds, bans roosters, and requires coops with at least four square feet per bird, set at least 15 feet from every property line. It bans selling chickens or eggs and requires a one-time permit.

Rentals on lots under half an acre won’t qualify, so this affects only larger parcels. City permission doesn’t override a lease. If you own on a larger parcel, decide whether you’ll allow chickens and say so in the lease’s animal section, including who’s responsible for the coop and for any damage when the resident moves out.

## North: Worthington

### Worthington: Comprehensive Plan Goes to City Council

***Status: Recommended for approval (awaiting Council vote). Staff recommended introducing and approving it the same night.***

Worthington City Council placed Resolution No. 59-2026, adopting the 2026 Worthington Together Comprehensive Plan, on its October 19, 2026 agenda. The Municipal Planning Commission unanimously recommended the plan on September 24, and staff recommended approval as presented. Council’s vote will appear in the approved minutes for that meeting.

The plan is the first full update since 2005. Its housing goals include enabling ADUs and small-scale housing such as two-family homes, fourplexes, townhomes, and cottage courts. Worthington is now seeking a consultant to rewrite its 1971 Planning and Zoning Code to match. Investors interested in adding an ADU to a [Worthington](/property-management-worthington-ohio/) lot, or in small multifamily there, should follow the code rewrite. That’s where lot sizes, parking, and approval steps get set.

[View Source Document](https://www.worthington.org/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6870). See also the city’s [request for proposals to rewrite the zoning code](https://planning.org/consultants/rfp/9336036/).

## Northwest: Plain City

### Plain City: Property Maintenance Code in the Works

***Status: Under discussion (no vote scheduled). The village hasn’t published a draft yet.***

At the October 21, 2026 Plain City Planning and Zoning meeting, the mayor responded to concerns about properties in the village that aren’t being kept up by saying the village is working on implementing a property maintenance code.

A property maintenance code sets minimum standards for exteriors, yards, structures, and sometimes interiors, with an enforcement process behind them. Plain City sits on the northwest edge of the Columbus metro, between Dublin and Marysville. If you own rentals there, a quick exterior walk-through now (gutters, siding, fences, peeling paint) puts you ahead of whatever standards the village adopts.
